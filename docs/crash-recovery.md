# Crash recovery

## What you'll see

If PandaCode crashes, it opens again by itself after about a second and a half and shows one notice in the conversation pane. The notice stays until you close it. It starts with one of these:

- "PandaCode crashed at 14:32 and restarted." The usual case. A crash that ends PandaCode before it can tell the guard, such as a stack overflow on a thread PandaCode did not start, is treated as a forced quit: no restart and no notice (see [Limits](#limits)).
- "PandaCode crashed at 14:32 and didn't restart." For example the restart failed, or you opened PandaCode yourself first. With automatic restarts off it says "PandaCode crashed at 14:32. Automatic restarts are off in Settings." instead, with a **Crash settings** button.
- "PandaCode kept crashing, last at 14:32, and didn't restart." After 3 crashes in 10 minutes, or 2 crashes within 30 s of launch with no other crash between them (within 10 minutes; a run that ended normally in between does not break the run), PandaCode stops restarting and you open it yourself. The notice then says when restarts resume, for example "Automatic restarts resume by 14:43.", and once that time has passed "Automatic restarts are on again."
- "PandaCode's window stopped and was reloaded." The app kept running but its window failed (macOS). When the reload brought everything back, nothing is shown. The two other wordings are "PandaCode's window stopped again and was reloaded." and "Last time PandaCode ran, its window failed."

Unsent text in the message box (also a shared bot channel's) usually comes back, without attachments, unless you turned off Settings → General → Crash recovery → **Keep unsent drafts** (on by default). Every change is written to the webview's local storage at once, but the webview may write that to disk slightly later, so the very last keystrokes before a hard crash can be missing (not tested in a real crash). The thread you had open comes back too, unless opening it may have caused the crash. Then it stays closed, and the notice names it and has an **Open thread** button. If agents were stopped, the notice says so: "Running agents were stopped; their threads show as Interrupted." **Show crash report** shows the report in your file manager.

PandaCode does not restart when you quit it (also for an update), when you force it to quit, when the system ends it, or after repeated crashes. You can turn restarts off in Settings → General → Crash recovery → **Restart automatically after a crash**. Crash reports stay on your computer; a report can contain file paths, text from your work or a credential that was in a panic message, so check it before sharing it.

The rest of this page is the internal reference.

## Overview

When PandaCode crashes it opens again by itself and says so once. A deliberate exit (Quit, an update restart, a termination signal, a launch that cannot finish its setup) or a silent kill is never followed by a relaunch. Crash reports are plain text files on this computer. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the feature makes no network request.

Code: `src-tauri/src/crash_guard.rs` and `src-tauri/src/crash_guard/` (native), `src/crash-recovery.ts`, `src/crash-recovery-model.ts`, `src/crash-restart-settings.ts`, `src/toast-keys.ts`, `src/toast-clearance.ts` (UI), `src/launch-restore.ts` (drafts and the open thread), `src/preview-crash.ts` (browser preview mocks).

## How it works

1. At startup `crash_guard::start` first removes old files (whether or not a guard will run): crash reports older than 90 days, the `serve-restart.log` and `crash-guard.log` files (and their `.1`) last written more than 90 days ago, crash times the loop limit no longer reads and notices older than 90 days from both modes' state files, and temp files a save left behind more than an hour ago (`prune_old_files`; see [Files](#files)). The same removal runs again about once a day while the process stays up. A `serve` on Unix whose stdout is `serve-restart.log` (a relaunched host) also starts its log size check here (see [Files](#files)). Then it spawns the guard. It runs no guard when PandaCode is process 1 (a container without an init, where nothing outlives the app to restart it) and logs "Crash guard is off: PandaCode runs as process 1, and nothing outlives it to restart it." The desktop app calls it first in `.setup` in `run()` (`lib.rs`), after plugin setup, so a duplicate launch has already handed off to the running instance through the single-instance plugin and exited without a guard of its own. When the rest of the desktop setup then fails (`setup_common`: a database newer than this build, a corrupt database, an unwritable data folder; or the agents dock or overlay setup), `crash_guard::desktop_startup_failed` (`startup.rs`) prints "PandaCode could not start: <error>" to stderr, announces a deliberate exit (`notify_exit`, before the dialog, so a logout or Force Quit while it waits is not a crash), shows a blocking native error dialog (`rfd`, run on the main thread) titled "PandaCode could not start" with an OK button, and exits with status 1 once it is closed. The dialog's body, under that title, is the error as a sentence (first letter capitalised, a full stop added when it has no closing punctuation; `startup::sentence`), a blank line, then "If this keeps happening, check PandaCode's data folder:" with the data folder's path on the next line (the error alone when no data folder could be worked out). The advice is the same for every error, since a setup error is plain text with no kind to tell causes apart. On Linux PandaCode shows no dialog of its own, since rfd's synchronous dialog would run a nested GTK loop inside tao's and can deadlock: the same body is printed to stderr after the first line, and then, as a best effort, the first of `zenity` (`--error --no-markup`), `kdialog` (`--error`) and `notify-send` found as an executable file in `/usr/bin` or `/bin` (checked in that order, never looked up on `PATH`, which whoever started PandaCode set; `wrapper::system_program`) is started with the title and a shorter text as plain arguments (no shell): "PandaCode could not start." (some of them never show the title) with the error sentence on the next line, without the data folder (`startup::note`). It is started detached and never waited for, and a failure is ignored; the app exits with status 1 at once. With none of the three installed only stderr says why. Tauri would otherwise turn the setup error into a panic, which the guard would relaunch into the same failure. Such an exit is never relaunched, writes no report and records no crash; a notice this launch took at its start goes back into the state file for the next launch (step 7). `headless.rs` calls it for `serve` and `serve --sign-in` only, once the app has been built and before its run loop starts (`headless::serve`): setup and everything the host does run inside the run loop, so they are guarded, while a failure to build the app is not. A `serve` that cannot start (the build or its setup fails) prints "pandacode serve could not start: <error>", announces a deliberate exit and exits with status 1, so it is never relaunched into the same failure. It spawns the same binary as `pandacode __crash-guard <pid> <data dir> <target> <mode> <launched at> armed|disarmed <wrapper or -> -- <app args>`. `main.rs` dispatches that subcommand first (`handle_crash_guard`): no GUI, no Tauri runtime. The guard runs in its own process group on Unix (`process_group(0)`), so a terminal Ctrl-C goes to the app only. On Windows it starts with `CREATE_NO_WINDOW | CREATE_NEW_PROCESS_GROUP | CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB`, and without breakaway if the job refuses it.
   The guard checks its arguments against what it works out itself: any local process can run `pandacode __crash-guard …`. Before watching it derives its own relaunch target (`relaunch::launch_target`), data directory (`app_data_dir`) and `xvfb-run` wrapper (`wrapper::for_mode`) from the same sources the app used, and when the arguments name a different one it logs "Crash guard: the <relaunch target | data directory | wrapper> in its arguments is not this app's; not running." and exits (`guard::run_args`, `disagreement`). This is a consistency check, not a security boundary: it stops the guard being pointed at another program or directory by its arguments alone. The values it derives come from its own environment (`$HOME`, `$XDG_DATA_HOME`, `$APPIMAGE`, `$APPDIR`, `XAUTHORITY`), which a caller running as the same user controls, so it does not protect against a process that already runs as that user.
2. The guard's stdin is a pipe from the app (`link.rs`). The app writes one ASCII line per message (`protocol.rs`). Sends never block the app (`writer.rs`): on Unix the write end is non-blocking and every line is shorter than `PIPE_BUF`, so a write is whole or fails; on Windows a writer thread drains a queue of 256 lines. A full pipe or queue (a stuck guard) drops the line. A dropped line marks the link dirty, and the next send (or the 15 s check below, whichever comes first) first resyncs: `reset`, one `child` line per current agent group, the live setting (`arm` or `disarm`), the focus line, `interrupted` while it applies (sent for the notice held now, which is not shown yet), the held notice's state (`held <…>` while one is held unshown, otherwise `shown`) and, once a deliberate exit was announced, `exit` again (`link::resync`), so a lost `unchild` never leaves the guard a stale group to kill, a lost `disarm` never lets it relaunch and a lost `exit` is resent. The link stays dirty until a whole resync goes through.
3. A thread in the app checks the guard every 15 s. If the guard exited without being told to, or a send finds the pipe broken, the app starts a new guard (at most 3 times per run, `MAX_RESPAWNS`) and replays the live setting, the agent process groups, the focus line, `interrupted` and `held` or `shown` (all as in a resync, `link::replay`; no guard is respawned after `exit`) and any termination signal already received. A respawned guard gets the relaunch target worked out when the first one started (`link::Setup`), not a new lookup. If starting it fails, it logs "Crash guard could not be restarted: <error>" and the next check tries again, within the same limit. The first guard is no different: when its spawn in `start` fails (`link::connect`, `first_link`), the app logs "Crash guard did not start (<error>); it is tried again shortly." and the checks retry it the same way, each try counting against the same 3, with the full replay once one starts. A send while a retry is pending does not spawn one itself; it counts as failed. The check runs every 15 s (`CHECK_INTERVAL`); when a line that must not wait (`shown`) fails, `link::repair_soon` also runs it at once and then every 250 ms, up to 20 times. Once the 3 are used up it logs "Crash guard keeps stopping; PandaCode runs without it until restarted." and tries no more in that run.
4. The pipe reaches EOF when the app is gone, however it went. While the pipe stays open the guard also checks the app's pid every 500 ms (`ALIVE_POLL`): a child that another thread spawned in the moment between the pipe's creation and its close-on-exec (macOS has no `pipe2`) can keep a write end open after the app died, so once the app is gone the guard reads for 200 ms more (`DRAIN`) and then treats the pipe as closed (`guard::pipe_lines`). Lines on the pipe are trusted as much as any process running as the same user: a process that inherited the write end this way could send lines of its own. The guard only bounds what such a writer can cost it: a line longer than 1024 bytes (`MAX_LINE`; the app's lines are under `PIPE_BUF`) is skipped without being held in memory (`guard::bounded_lines`), and it tracks at most 4096 agent groups (`MAX_CHILDREN`), ignoring new ones beyond that (they are then not stopped after a crash). Then the guard waits for the process to be gone (at most 10 s, `GONE_TIMEOUT`). On Unix a zombie counts as gone (a parent that reaps late must not stop recovery), and so does a pid that now names a later process; when the process details cannot be read it falls back to `kill(pid, 0)`, so a failed read never declares a live app gone (`Process::gone`). If the app is still alive after that wait, the guard logs "Crash guard: PandaCode is still running after its pipe closed; leaving its agents alone and not relaunching." and exits: no agent group is stopped, no report or notice is written. Otherwise it stops the agent process groups the app reported, classifies the death, writes a report if needed, applies the loop limit, saves the notice, waits 1.5 s (`RELAUNCH_DELAY`) and relaunches the app with `--crash-restart=<crash time in ms>` (see [What the guard does after a crash](#what-the-guard-does-after-a-crash)).
5. The app tells the guard whether PandaCode is in front: `focus` while any of its windows (the main window, the tray popover, the launcher, the agents dock …) has focus, `blur` once none has (`crash_guard::window_focused`, from `on_run_event` on each window's `Focused` event; a `Destroyed` window counts as losing focus, since a closed window may never send its blur). It keeps the set of focused windows, so the order in which two windows report a focus change does not matter, and sends a line only when the state changes, and again with every resync. On macOS a bundle relaunch then stays in the background when the last line was `blur`; without any focus line it comes to the front (`guard::relaunch_in_background`).
6. When the app announces a deliberate exit (`exit`), the app no longer respawns a guard, and a line that then finds the guard gone is not a failure. What the guard does differs by system (`guard::LEAVE_AT_EXIT`):
   - Windows: the guard quits at once, so it never keeps the executable open while an installer replaces it during an update restart (the app's job objects end its agents anyway).
   - macOS and Linux: replacing a running executable is fine there, so the guard stays until the pipe closes, as after any other death: it waits for the app to be gone (at most 10 s), stops every agent process group still registered (a crash or kill during a shutdown that takes seconds must not orphan them; after a normal shutdown their `unchild` lines have already removed them) and then, the death being deliberate, writes nothing and starts nothing. If the app is still alive 10 s after its pipe closed, it logs the still-running line of step 4 and stops nothing. An update restart is no different: the old guard watches the old process to its end, and the new process starts its own guard.

   A `term` line does not end the watch: if the app outlives the signal, the guard is still there.

   Which quits announce it (`on_run_event` in `lib.rs`): every path that calls `app.exit(0)` or asks for a restart reaches `RunEvent::ExitRequested`, and the first of those sends `exit` (`notify_exit`) and then the `app-quitting` event to the main webview, before agents are given up to 12 s to stop. That is the app menu's Quit and Cmd+Q on macOS, the menu bar item's Quit (its menu and the tray popover's `tray_quit`), closing the main window where that quits (Linux, or no menu bar item), an update restart (`relaunch()` from `@tauri-apps/plugin-process`) and the window dialog's **Restart PandaCode** (`request_restart`). On macOS PandaCode sets Tauri's default menu with its Quit item swapped for one of the same title and Cmd+Q that calls `app.exit(0)` (`app_menu.rs`), because the default Quit sends `terminate:`, which tao turns straight into `RunEvent::Exit` without `ExitRequested`. Anything else that sends `terminate:` still does so: the Dock's Quit and a logout end in `RunEvent::Exit` only. There `notify_exit` is still sent (a deliberate exit, never relaunched), and `app-quitting` is emitted only when no `ExitRequested` shutdown has begun, but the page may already be past handling it, and agents get no 12 s to stop (`providers::shutdown_all` ends them). See [Limits](#limits).
7. The notice waits in its mode's state file for the next launch: the desktop app's guard writes `crash-guard.json`, a `serve` host's guard writes `crash-guard-serve.json` (`state::state_path`), so a desktop app and a `serve` host on one data directory never take each other's notice or count each other's crashes. The next launch takes it out of the file in `crash_guard::start`, right after spawning its own guard (`take_notice`, `state::take_pending`; the crash history stays, less any crash time more than 10 minutes old or dated after now, which the loop limit no longer reads). Since `start` runs in `.setup`, after the single-instance plugin, a duplicate launch never takes it. Taking it holds the state file's lock (see [Files](#files)); a launch that cannot get the lock within 2 s (`LOCK_WAIT`) logs "Could not take the crash notice: …" and takes nothing, so the notice stays for a later launch. When the cleared file cannot be saved (a full disk: the save needs a new temp file) it logs "Could not clear the crash notice: …" and removes the state file instead, which needs no space: the notice is taken, and the crash history goes with it (the loop limit starts counting again). When even that fails (no write access to the folder) it logs "Could not remove the crash notice either, so it is not shown: …": the notice is not handed out (it would come back at every launch), and the process keeps the lock until it exits (`state::clear_by_removing`, `keep_lock`), so a guard still in its pause cannot replace the notice and starts nothing beside this launch (step 5 of [What the guard does after a crash](#what-the-guard-does-after-a-crash)); any other process of that mode waits up to 2 s for the lock and fails the same way. This process's own later attempts on that state file (the daily prune) fail at once, logged, instead of waiting 2 s on the lock it keeps. A desktop run set up for a guard keeps the notice in memory (`commands::hold_notice`), and `crash_recovery_take` gives the main webview the same notice on every call until the page reports with `crash_recovery_shown` that the notice is on screen; after that it answers `null` for the rest of the run. So a page that dies (or is reloaded) before the notice shows gets it again on the next load, and a reload after it was shown gets nothing. When a deliberate exit comes before the page reported it shown (the user quit before the page booted, or the setup failed), `notify_exit` writes the held notice back into `crash-guard.json` (`commands::put_back_unshown`, `state::put_back_pending`, under the lock, keeping the crash history), so the next launch shows it; it does this once per run, and not when a notice of the same or a later crash is already waiting there. A failure is logged ("Could not keep the crash notice for the next launch: …") and the notice is lost. The guard also knows the held notice: `hold_notice` sends it as a `held` line (resent by every resync) and `crash_recovery_shown` sends `shown`. When the app ends without announcing an exit and its death is not a crash (SIGTERM, SIGINT, SIGHUP or SIGQUIT, or a silent kill such as SIGKILL or Force Quit) while a notice is held and not yet shown, the guard writes it back (`guard::put_back_held`, `state::put_back_pending`, keeping a notice of the same or a later crash that already waits), so the next launch shows it. A failure there is logged in `crash-guard.log` ("Crash guard could not keep the unshown crash notice: …"). The notice stays held after this: a page that finishes booting during the quit still gets it, and when it then reports it shown, `crash_recovery_shown` removes the copy it put back from `crash-guard.json` again (`state::remove_pending`, only a notice of that same crash, under the lock), so the next launch does not show it a second time; a failure there is logged ("Could not clear the crash notice kept for the next launch: …") and the next launch shows it again. A desktop run not set up for a guard (the guard is off, or no relaunch target could be worked out) drops the notice, and a run whose guard only failed to spawn still hands it out. The frontend asks once per page load, early in boot, before it reopens a thread, and shows it in the toast once boot has chosen the thread to open. A `serve` host has no webview: `start` prints a line instead (see Relaunch).

The `--crash-restart` flag is filtered out of the args the app and `serve` parse (`is_restart_flag`).

### Pipe messages

| Line | Sent by | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `exit` | `notify_exit`: `on_run_event` on `ExitRequested` (first time) and `Exit`; `headless::startup_failed`; `crash_guard::desktop_startup_failed`; resent by every resync after it was announced | Deliberate exit follows (Quit, update restart, `serve` shutdown, a desktop app or `serve` that could not start). The guard leaves at once on Windows; on Unix it watches until EOF and stops agent groups still registered |
| `term <signo>` | Unix signal handler for SIGTERM, SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGQUIT | A death within 60 s (`TERM_GRACE_MS`) is deliberate |
| `signal <signo>` | Unix signal handler for SIGSEGV, SIGBUS, SIGILL, SIGFPE, SIGABRT, SIGTRAP, SIGSYS | Crash, if the app dies within 10 minutes (`FATAL_GRACE_MS`) |
| `panic <summary>` | Panic hook | Crash, with the same 10-minute limit; summary is one line of at most 240 bytes |
| `report` | Panic hook | A panic report was written |
| `arm` / `disarm` | `set_crash_restart_enabled` | The Settings switch; the guard decides from the last one received |
| `child <pid> <start time>` / `unchild <pid>` | `ProcessGroup` in `providers.rs` | Agent process group started or ended, with its leader's start time |
| `reset` | `link.rs` resync | Forget every agent group (not `interrupted` or the held notice); the app's current `child` lines, its setting (`arm`/`disarm`), its focus line, `interrupted` while it applies, `held` or `shown`, and, once announced, `exit` follow (after a dropped line, and on a respawned guard) |
| `interrupted` | `crash_guard::store_opened`, after the database was opened at startup (`setup_common`), only when a notice is held unshown and it says agents were stopped (this run's database recovery marked at least one non-archived task `restart_interrupted`, or the held notice already said so); resent by every resync until the page reports the notice shown | Agents were stopped by the crash whose notice is held unshown: a notice the guard saves (or puts back) for a death of this run before that notice is shown says agents were stopped (`agentsInterrupted`). It belongs only to the notice held now: `shown` clears it in the guard and `crash_recovery_shown` stops the app resending it (`link::clear_interrupted`), so a later crash never carries it, and with no notice held it is never sent |
| `focus` / `blur` | `crash_guard::window_focused`, from `on_run_event` on `WindowEvent::Focused` and `Destroyed` | Some PandaCode window has focus (`focus`) or none has (`blur`); decides whether a macOS relaunch comes to the front |
| `held <crashed_at> <flags> <report or -> [cause]` | `commands::hold_notice` at setup; resent by every resync while the notice is unshown | The desktop app holds this notice for its page. `flags` is four 0/1 digits: hasReport, restartsPaused, restarted, agentsInterrupted. Only the report's file name and the closed-set cause are sent. If the app then dies without `exit` and the death is not a crash, the guard writes the notice back for the next launch |
| `shown` | `crash_recovery_shown`; resent by every resync once the notice was shown (or when none is held) | No notice is held unshown any more; the guard also drops `interrupted` |
| `ping` | Nothing sends it today | Ignored |

Malformed or cut-off lines are ignored. The first fatal cause wins: a panic followed by SIGABRT (release builds use `panic = "abort"`) is reported as the panic. A `child` pid must be between 2 and `i32::MAX`, so it can never name the guard's own group or every process.

The signal handlers only use async-signal-safe calls (`write(2)`, `sigaction`, `raise`). After writing `signal N`, the handler sets that signal back to its default action (`SIG_DFL`, not the action installed before ours) and re-raises it, so an announced fatal signal always ends the process, also one sent with `kill`. The previous action is deliberately not chained: Rust's stack-overflow handler treats a SIGSEGV or SIGBUS without a guard-page address (one sent with `kill`) as not its own and returns, which used to leave the app running while the guard held `signal N`. Its "thread overflowed its stack" line is not printed this way. After writing `term N`, the handler restores the previous action and re-raises; if another handler (tokio's in `serve`) chained to ours, a termination signal is left to that handler. A termination signal that was ignored when the app started (`nohup`'s SIGHUP, a shell's SIGINT and SIGQUIT for background jobs) is left ignored, so it never becomes a `term` line and a death.

The panic hook first sends `panic` (before it captures the backtrace, so the guard learns of the panic even if capturing it or writing the report goes wrong), then writes the report and sends `report`, then calls the previous hook. On Unix both lines are written straight to the pipe's file descriptor (`signals::write_raw`), never through the link's lock, which another thread (the 15 s check, an agent's `child` line) or the panicking thread itself may hold. On Windows they go through the link with `try_lock` and are skipped when the lock is taken. It is installed only when the guard is set up. A panic inside the hook (or on another thread while it runs) goes straight to the previous hook.

In a macOS build with `panic = "abort"` (release builds), once the previous hook has printed and both lines (`panic`, then `report` after the report was written) reached the pipe, the hook ends the process with `_exit(101)` instead of the abort (`panic_hook::exit_quietly`), so macOS shows no "quit unexpectedly" dialog for a panic. The guard classifies the death from the `panic` line and stops agent groups as after any crash. The cost: ReportCrash writes no `.ips` log and no core dump for a panic; PandaCode's own panic report is the record. When either write failed or no guard runs, the normal abort follows. Unwinding builds (tests, debug) and other systems never take this path.

## Crash or deliberate exit

`policy::classify`, from what the pipe said and what the OS reports:

| Situation | Result | Relaunched |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `exit` received, or `term N` within the last 60 s | Deliberate | No |
| `panic …` within the last 10 minutes | Crash, `Panic: <summary>` | Yes, if armed and the loop limit allows |
| `signal N` within the last 10 minutes | Crash, `Crashed with SIGSEGV` etc. | Yes, if armed and the loop limit allows. A crash whose signal handler cannot run (a stack overflow on a thread without an alternate signal stack, such as one of WebKit's) sends no `signal N` and is a kill (next rows; see [Limits](#limits)) |
| Windows exit code `0xC000013A` (console closed with Ctrl-C) or `0xC000026B` (logoff) | Deliberate | No |
| Other Windows exit codes with NTSTATUS error bits (`0xC…`): access violation, illegal instruction, stack overflow, `__fastfail`, heap corruption | Crash, `Crashed with an access violation` etc. | Yes, if armed and the loop limit allows |
| Nothing on the pipe, macOS/Linux (SIGKILL, Force Quit, the Linux OOM killer, macOS jetsam, a crash whose signal handler cannot run) | Killed | No |
| Nothing on the pipe, Windows exit code 0 or 1 (an exit the app chose, or Task Manager's `TerminateProcess`) | Killed | No |
| Windows exit code above 1 without error bits | Deliberate | No |

The limits mean an old marker no longer explains a death: a SIGTERM more than 60 s before a crash does not hide the crash, and a marker the app survived (a `panic` line from a caught panic in a debug build with the guard forced on) followed by a silent kill more than 10 minutes later is a silent kill. The fatal limit is much longer than the termination one because after `signal N` the handler re-raises into the default action, and the pipe only closes once the kernel, systemd-coredump or ReportCrash has finished the core dump, which for a large process on a slow disk can take minutes. The app cannot outlive a `signal N` it sent, since the handler always re-raises into the default action. A survived `panic` line followed by a silent kill within 10 minutes is misread as a crash.

A silent kill is never relaunched on purpose: a non-parent cannot learn why a Unix process died, and a SIGKILL is usually the user's or the system's decision. A Killed or Deliberate death writes no report and leaves no notice.

## What the guard does after a crash

`guard::conclude`, after a death classified as a crash. The crash time (`guard::crash_time`) is when the first fatal line (`panic` or `signal`) arrived, kept between this run's launch and now, or now when there is none (a Windows exception): the notice's `crashedAt`, the crash record, the guard's report (its name and header) and the relaunch's `--crash-restart=<ms>` all carry it, so a slow core dump or wait does not move it later.

1. Writes a `crash` report unless the panic hook already reported this death: a `report` line arrived, or, if that line was lost, the guard adopts the hook's panic report (`guard::panic_hook_reported`). It adopts one only when the death can be a panic (`policy::may_be_panic`: a `panic` line arrived, the fatal signal was SIGABRT, or the Windows exit code was `0xC0000409`, the abort that follows a panic) and a panic report written by the guarded process is dated no earlier than 60 s before the crash time (`PANIC_REPORT_SLACK_MS`) and no earlier than this run's launch (`report::panic_report_since`). Panic reports are checked newest first: one dated more than a day after now (`FUTURE_SLACK_MS`) is skipped, the search stops at the first one dated before that lower limit, and the first one whose header has the line `Process: <pid>` with the app's pid is adopted (`report::written_by`, which reads only the header lines before the first blank line, so a panic message cannot fake it). A desktop app and a `serve` host share `crash-reports/`, so this keeps one from adopting the other's panic report. When the `report` line arrived, the hook's report is found the same way with only this run's launch as the lower limit. A death by another signal or exception never adopts a panic report, and neither does a report an earlier run wrote (a panic, a relaunch, then another crash within the 60 s). So one death leaves one report: the hook's panic report or the guard's `crash` report, never both. When no hook report is found (also when the `report` line arrived but its file is gone), the guard writes its own `crash` report. `hasReport` is true exactly when the guard found or wrote a report for this death, whose file name it keeps in the notice's `report`; if its own report cannot be written, the death leaves none and the notice has `hasReport: false`.
2. If the live setting is off, it saves a notice with `restarted: false` and `notRestarted: "off"`, does not record the crash in the history and starts nothing. The live setting is `crash-restart.json`, read again at this moment (`state::saved_setting`): the file is shared by every process on the data directory, so a switch flipped in the desktop app also decides for a `serve` guard there, which never hears the desktop's `arm`/`disarm`. When the file is missing (the switch was never changed) or cannot be read, the last `arm`/`disarm` line decides, starting from the value at spawn.
3. Otherwise it records the crash (a startup crash when its first fatal line arrived within 30 s of launch, so a slow core dump does not move it later) and applies the loop limit. When restarts pause it saves a notice with `restartsPaused: true` and starts nothing.
4. Otherwise it saves the crash history with a notice that says `restarted: false`, then waits 1.5 s (`RELAUNCH_DELAY`). A guard killed during the wait (a logout) has counted the crash and starts nothing, and the next launch does not claim a restart.
5. It then saves the notice marked `relaunching: true`, still with `restarted: false` (before launching, since the new process may read it at once), and relaunches with `--crash-restart=<crash time>`. Only the launch carrying that mark turns the notice into `restarted: true` when it takes it (`state::Pending::confirmed`, called from `take_notice` with `relaunch::restart_mark`); any other launch that takes it (one opened by hand, or the next launch after the relaunch died before taking it) shows it as not restarted. So a notice never says "restarted" unless the relaunched app came up far enough to take it, on every system and in both modes, including a macOS bundle opened through `open -n` and a `serve` relaunch. If the relaunch fails it puts the `restarted: false` notice back with `notRestarted: "failed"`. A relaunch the guard starts itself (everything but a macOS bundle) is watched for 2 s (`relaunch::STARTED_WATCH`, `still_running`): one that has exited by then died before it could take the notice or start its own guard (a `serve` whose app build fails, a desktop launch that dies in plugin setup), so it counts as failed and the guard logs "Crash guard could not relaunch PandaCode: the relaunched app exited at once (<status>)". One that exited by then with status 0 is most likely the single-instance handoff to a PandaCode the user opened meanwhile (`Outcome::HandedOff`): the guard logs "Crash guard: the relaunched app handed off to a running PandaCode." and handles it like a failed relaunch, putting the not-relaunching notice back unless the running PandaCode already took it (that launch carries no mark, so it shows the notice as not restarted). A `serve` relaunch that exits at once with status 0 is logged the same way. A macOS bundle opened through `/usr/bin/open` has no process handle here; only `open`'s own exit status is checked, and the notice says restarted only if that launch takes it. Both saves replace the notice only while it is still the one the guard saved (`state::replace_pending`). When the user opens PandaCode of the same mode by hand during the wait, that launch's `start` takes the notice out of the state file during its setup (step 7 of [How it works](#how-it-works)), long before its window asks for it; the guard then finds the notice gone, saves nothing, starts nothing and logs "Crash guard: PandaCode was opened again during the pause; not relaunching." (`Outcome::AlreadyRunning`). When that launch could neither clear nor remove the notice, it still holds the state file's lock: the guard's save fails after 2 s, it logs "Crash guard could not save its state: another PandaCode process is changing the crash state" and starts nothing (`Outcome::Paused`), leaving the waiting notice in the file. The manual launch shows the not-restarted notice (a desktop run set up for a guard), drops it (a desktop run without one) or prints it (`serve`); either way the crash stays counted and no stale notice is left for a later launch. A launch of the other mode (a `serve` host while a desktop guard waits, or the reverse) takes nothing, so the guard relaunches as usual. A launch whose setup comes after the guard's swap is a second start beside the relaunched one (a desktop launch then hands off to whichever instance came first).

Every save holds the state file's lock and re-reads the file first (`state::update`, `state::replace_pending`), so a record another writer saved meanwhile is kept; a save that cannot get the lock within 2 s fails. The loop limit fails closed: if either save in steps 4 or 5 fails, the guard starts nothing (`Outcome::Paused`), since without a saved history the limit could not stop a crash loop. When the first save (the crash history with its notice) fails, nothing is saved: the guard logs "Crash guard could not save its state: <error>" and then "Crash guard could not record the crash, so PandaCode was not restarted." (`Outcome::StateUnsaved`), starts nothing and leaves no notice. With the switch off a failed save is logged the same first way and leaves no notice either. So every crash whose state could be saved leaves a notice for the next launch, and the notice says it restarted only when the launch the guard started took it.

The notice keeps only a cause from a closed set (`policy::public_cause`): `Panic`, `Crashed with <signal name>` for a known signal, or `Crashed with <Windows exception phrase>`; anything else is dropped. Panic text and paths stay in the report. `crash_recovery_take` filters a notice an older guard saved the same way.

## Crash-loop protection

Constants in `crash_guard/policy.rs`:

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CRASH_WINDOW_MS` | 10 min | Crashes in this window count |
| `MAX_CRASHES_IN_WINDOW` | 3 | The 3rd crash in the window pauses restarts |
| `STARTUP_GRACE_MS` | 30 s | A crash whose first fatal line arrived this soon after launch is a startup crash |
| `MAX_STARTUP_CRASHES` | 2 | The 2 newest crash records in the window both being startup crashes pauses restarts |
| `HISTORY_LIMIT` | 10 | Crash records kept in each state file |

"In a row" counts crash records only (`policy::verdict`: the newest records in the window that are all startup crashes). Only a crash recorded with the switch on makes a record, so a healthy run that ended normally between two startup crashes leaves no record and does not break the run: two startup crashes within 10 minutes pause restarts even with a normal quit between them. Only a crash later than 30 s after its launch in between breaks it.

Recording a crash keeps only the records from the 10 minutes before it (the only ones the limit reads), at most 10; records dated after it (a clock set back) are dropped. A crash while the switch is off is not recorded and leaves the history as it was. Taking the notice at a start of that mode (`take_notice` in `start`, with or without a guard) also drops the times more than 10 minutes old or dated after now (`state::take_pending`, `policy::in_window`), and so does the prune of both modes' state files at every desktop or `serve` host start and about once a day while one runs (`state::prune_old`; see [Files](#files)), so no crash time outlives the window by more than about a day while PandaCode runs. A notice cleared by removing the state file (see step 7 of [How it works](#how-it-works)) takes the whole history with it. Restarts resume by themselves once the crashes are older than the window.

### Web content process (macOS)

When WKWebView's web content process dies (a crash, or macOS ending it under memory pressure), that window goes blank while the app keeps running. `Builder::on_web_content_process_terminate` calls `crash_guard::webview::on_terminate`, which reloads the webview up to `MAX_WEB_RELOADS` (3) times within `WEB_CRASH_WINDOW_MS` (60 s). Each webview has its own budget, so in-app browser webviews never use up the main window's. Terminations of the app's own webviews (`main`, `tray`, `launcher`, `agents-dock`) write a `webview` report when the guard was set up; browser webviews write none.

When the main window's budget runs out it is not reloaded again, and a dialog titled "PandaCode needs to restart" says: "PandaCode's window keeps failing. Restarting usually fixes it but stops running agents. Not Now keeps the window blank until you quit and reopen." (`RESTART_MESSAGE`) with **Restart PandaCode** (a normal restart through the quit path, announced as a deliberate exit, so the guard does not count it as a crash) and **Not Now**, which leaves the blank window as it is. Other webviews are simply left blank. This runs whatever the Settings switch is set to. What the reloaded page, or the launch after **Restart PandaCode**, does about the thread that was open is in [What a launch restores](#what-a-launch-restores).

## Orphaned agents

`ProcessGroup::register` reports each agent process group (`child <pid> <start time>`) and its `Drop` reports `unchild`. Once the app is gone, however it died (also after `exit` or `term`, where a normal shutdown has usually ended the groups and sent their `unchild` lines already), the guard sends SIGKILL to each group still registered on Unix, but only when the group is still the app's: when the leader's start time is unchanged, or, when the leader is gone, when one of the group's current members started between the registered leader start and the app's death (`process::group_is_ours`). A reused pid is skipped, a group whose id was reused by a process started after the app died is skipped, and a group whose start times could not be read is never killed. The leaderless check has a known gap (see [Limits](#limits)). Group members are listed with `proc_listpgrppids` on macOS and from `/proc` on Linux. Start times come from `proc_pidinfo` on macOS and `/proc/<pid>/stat` on Linux. On Windows the app's job objects already end those trees when its handles close. The restarted app marks those runs Interrupted, as after any quit. Opening the database marks each task that was running or waiting (and that the user had not stopped, and is not a machine thread run by another host) `restart_interrupted` (`storage.rs`). It counts those tasks first, leaving out archived ones, which the page shows no Interrupted badge for (`Store::restart_interrupted`), and `crash_guard::store_opened` passes the count on. The held notice keeps whether agents were stopped (`Pending::agents_interrupted`, `commands::note_interrupted`): true when the count is above zero or the notice already said so. When it is true the app also sends `interrupted` to its guard (and again with every resync, until the page reports the notice shown), so a crash of this run before the page showed the notice saves a new notice that still says agents were stopped. With no notice held nothing is sent, and once the notice is shown both the app (`link::clear_interrupted`) and the guard (on `shown`) drop the flag, so a later crash never says agents were stopped because of it; the guard also keeps the flag of an unshown notice it replaces in the state file (`guard::conclude`). A notice put back at a deliberate exit carries it too, merged with the flag of any notice already waiting there (`state::put_back_pending`). `agentsInterrupted` in the IPC answer is the held notice's flag or a count above zero (`commands::recovery`). How the page uses it is in [The notice](#the-notice).

Old files are removed by `prune_old_files` (`crash_guard.rs`): at a desktop or `serve` host startup (the first thing `start` does, also when the guard is off) and then about once a day while that process runs (`PRUNE_EVERY_MS`, 24 h). With a guard, the app's 15 s check thread asks whether a day has passed (`link.rs`, `prune_if_due`); without one (the guard is off or did not start) a `crash-files-prune` thread asks every hour (`keep_pruning`, `PRUNE_CHECK`), so a `serve` host that systemd keeps up for months is covered too. Each run removes:

- crash reports past their count or age (the `crash-reports/` row above);
- both logs and their `.1` files, once last written more than 90 days ago, or more than a day after now (`FUTURE_SLACK_MS`: a clock that ran ahead, which would otherwise never age out) (`files::prune_logs`). A log that is the pruning process's own stdout or stderr is never removed (a relaunched `serve` host's `serve-restart.log`): it would go on writing into a deleted file. On Unix a log another running process writes to is not removed either: a relaunched `serve` host (in its 30 s log check) and a guard whose stderr is `crash-guard.log` (hourly, `guard::keep_log_fresh`) set their own open log's modification time to now through the handle, at most once a day (`files::refresh_output`, `LOG_FRESH_WITHIN`), so no prune ever sees a log in use as 90 days unwritten. Should one be deleted anyway, the host and the guard open it again, as described in the table above. On Windows there is no such refresh;
- from both state files (`crash-guard.json`, `crash-guard-serve.json`, each under its lock; `state::prune_old`): crash times more than 10 minutes old or dated after now, and a pending notice whose crash is more than 90 days old or dated more than a day after now (`FUTURE_SLACK_MS`). A state file that does not exist is left alone. A failure is logged ("Could not remove old crash times: …");
- temp files a process that died in the middle of a save left beside the three JSON files (`<file>.<pid>.tmp`), once last changed more than an hour ago or more than a day after now (`FUTURE_SLACK_MS`) (`files::prune_temp_files`, `STALE_TEMP_AGE`). A younger one may belong to a save in progress. Only those exact names are touched, and a symlink there is removed, not followed.

A `serve` settings command (which exits by itself) never calls `start`, so it removes nothing.

On Unix every one of these files is owner-only (0600) and `crash-reports/` is 0700 (`FILE_MODE`, `DIR_MODE`, set only on Unix), narrowed even when the file already existed with wider permissions (`files.rs`). A data folder the crash-guard code has to create (spawning the guard on a first run creates it for `crash-guard.log`, and a save creates a missing parent) is made with mode 0700, as is every missing parent folder (`files::create_dirs`; the process umask can only narrow it); a data folder that already exists keeps its mode, since the rest of the app shares it, except on Linux: there every desktop or `serve` start (`setup_common` in `lib.rs`) makes the webview's data folder, Tauri's app local data directory, owner-only (0700, `files::narrow_webview_data`, creating it if missing), and on Linux that folder is the app data folder itself. A symlink there is left alone and the failure logged ("Could not make the webview data folder private: <name> is a symlink"); startup goes on. On Windows PandaCode sets no permissions on these files or folders: they inherit the permissions of the profile folder they are in (`%APPDATA%`). Symlinks at the file itself are never followed:

- Writes open with `O_NOFOLLOW` on Unix. The lock files open the same way and must be regular files. The JSON files are written atomically: a stale temp file (or a link planted in its place) is removed, the temp file is created with `O_EXCL`, then renamed over the file. `crash-reports/` is refused when it is a symlink. `serve-restart.log` and `crash-guard.log` are size-checked with `symlink_metadata` before they are rotated, opened with `O_NOFOLLOW | O_NONBLOCK` (a FIFO planted at the path cannot block the open) and refused unless they are regular files (`files::open_private_log`).
- On Windows the same opens (report and temp-file writes, the logs, the lock files) use `FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT`, so a symlink or junction at the path is opened as itself, and are then refused unless they are regular files (`files::regular_blocking`). Like all Windows code here, this has never run.
- Reads of the JSON files open with `O_NOFOLLOW | O_NONBLOCK` on Unix (so a planted FIFO cannot block) and `FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT` on Windows, and accept only a regular file. A missing, corrupt, linked or oversized (over 64 KB) file reads as the default.
- Listing reports (the newest one, pruning, the panic-report check) and revealing a named report refuse a `crash-reports/` that is itself a symlink (`report::is_real_dir`): it lists as empty, so nothing is pruned or revealed through it and a reveal says there is no crash report. Listing reads each entry's own type, so a symlink in `crash-reports/` is never a report.

Only the last path part is protected: a symlinked parent directory is followed.

A panic report holds the PandaCode version, OS and architecture, time, process ID (`Process: <pid>`, which the guard uses to find it), thread name, source location, panic message and backtrace. In the header's thread name and source location every ASCII control character (CR, LF, tab and the rest) becomes a space (`report::header_value`), so neither can end its line and forge another, such as `Process:`. A guard report holds the same header (without the process ID) and the cause ("Cause: …", then "No panic was reported; the cause comes from the crash guard."). After a panic whose hook report was not found, that cause is "Panic: " and the panic message as the `panic` line carried it: one line, shortened to at most 240 bytes. So a guard report can hold the same kind of text as a panic report. Reports hold no environment variables and no settings. A panic message can still contain whatever text the panicking code put in it, such as a file path, text from the user's work or a credential (a token or password that was in the message); nothing is redacted. That is why Settings asks the user to check a report before sharing it. That text stays in the report: the IPC answer and `crash-guard.json` carry only the closed-set cause.

## Settings and environment

- Settings → General → Crash recovery → **Restart automatically after a crash** (on by default), described as "If PandaCode crashes, it restarts itself, pausing after repeated crashes. It won't restart if you force it to quit or the system ends it." (`CRASH_RESTART_DETAIL`). The switch shows what `crash_restart_enabled` answers. Until the answer arrives it shows neither state (`aria-busy` and `aria-disabled`, not `disabled`, so **Crash settings** can still focus it; a click or Space changes nothing), so a user who turned restarts off never sees it flash on. The answer is the value in `crash-restart.json` as it is at that moment, which another process on the same data directory may have changed, and only when that file is missing or cannot be read the value this process loaded at startup or last set (on by default; `commands::shown_setting`). A change is stored in `crash-restart.json` and sent to the running guard at once, and a crash notice on screen is rewritten for it (see [The notice](#the-notice)); the guard also reads the file again when the app dies, so a `serve` host on the same data directory follows the switch too. With the switch off the guard still runs, writes reports and leaves a notice, but it does not relaunch or record the crash in the history. If saving fails the switch goes back and shows "Could not change automatic restarts: …"; if reading fails it shows on and "Could not read the automatic restart setting: …".
- Below it, **Crash reports** ("Written when PandaCode crashes and kept on this computer. A report can contain file paths, text from your work or credentials, so check it before attaching it to a GitHub issue.", tied to the button with `aria-describedby`) has a **Show latest crash report** button (`SHOW_REPORTS`) that calls `crash_report_reveal`, the same command as the notice's **Show crash report**: it shows the report of the crash notice this run took, while that report exists and is not dated more than a day ahead, and otherwise the newest panic or crash report (see [How it behaves](#how-it-behaves)). With no report it says "There are no crash reports on this computer." in a status line that clears itself after 5 s (`STATUS_CLEAR_MS`); another failure shows "Could not show the crash report: …" (in the group's alert line). A report pruned between finding it and revealing it counts as no report (`reveal_error`).
- Below it, **Keep unsent drafts** (`KEEP_DRAFTS`, `#crash-drafts-keep`, on by default), a switch described by the note below it. Its choice is stored in the webview's local storage, `pandacode.keepDrafts` (`KEEP_DRAFTS_KEY`): `off` while off, removed when on (`PersistedDrafts.setKeeping`; a failed write is logged, "Could not save the Keep unsent drafts setting:", and the choice then holds for this launch only). Turned off, nothing is written any more and every stored `pandacode.draft:` key is removed at once, but text in any composer stays in memory (and on screen) for the running launch; at each launch while off every stored draft is removed and none is restored. Turned on again, each draft is stored from its next change. While off the note says "Unsent messages are not saved; a crash or quit loses them." (`DRAFTS_OFF_DETAIL`).
- Below the rows, a note under the group (`settings-group-note`) says, while Keep unsent drafts is on, "The text of unsent messages (not attachments or very long drafts) is saved locally as plain text, so a crash rarely loses it. A pasted secret is kept too, until the message is sent, cleared, or 30 days old." (`DRAFTS_KEPT_DETAIL`; the number comes from `DRAFT_MAX_AGE`). The parenthesis covers attachments, which are never kept, and drafts over 50,000 UTF-16 code units, which are not stored, without naming the number (see [What a launch restores](#what-a-launch-restores)). "Rarely loses it" is not a guarantee: the last keystrokes before a hard crash can be missing (see Drafts there). Beside it, **Clear saved drafts** (`CLEAR_DRAFTS`, described by the note) removes every stored draft except that text in an open composer stays on screen, unstored until its next change, and confirms with the toast "Saved drafts cleared." (see Clearing there).
- Searching Settings for crash, restart, recovery, report, drafts, unsent messages or secret finds the General page (`settings-sections.ts` keywords).
- `PANDACODE_NO_CRASH_GUARD=1` turns the guard off in any build (any value other than empty or `0`).
- `PANDACODE_CRASH_GUARD=1` turns it on in debug builds, where it is off by default.
- `serve` runs no guard when systemd runs it as a service (`systemd::supervised`), since systemd restarts the service itself and a guard beside it would start a second host. That is the case when `INVOCATION_ID` is set and not empty and, beside it, either `JOURNAL_STREAM` (`<device>:<inode>`) names this process's own stdout or stderr, or (Linux) the parent process is named `systemd` (read from `/proc/<parent pid>/comm`). The parent-name rule applies only to a `serve` started without a `--crash-restart` flag: a host the guard relaunched is re-parented to `systemd` (a user manager is a subreaper) once the guard exits, and the guard removed `INVOCATION_ID` and `JOURNAL_STREAM` from its environment anyway (see [Relaunch](#relaunch)), so a relaunched host always gets a guard. `INVOCATION_ID` alone is not enough: everything a service starts inherits it, so a `pandacode serve` typed into a terminal that itself runs as a systemd user service has it too, and that one gets a guard. The log line is "Crash guard is off: systemd runs this serve as a service, and restarts are left to its Restart= setting."
- `serve` under `xvfb-run` (detected on Linux by `XAUTHORITY` pointing into an `xvfb-run.` temp directory) needs `xvfb-run` in `/usr/bin` or `/bin` (never looked up on `PATH`; `wrapper::system_program`) to start a new display for the relaunch; without it the guard is off and logs "Crash guard is off: serve runs under xvfb-run, but no xvfb-run in /usr/bin or /bin can start a new display."

What is logged (to stderr) when a run gets no guard: nothing when the build or the environment variables turn it off (a debug build, `PANDACODE_NO_CRASH_GUARD`); otherwise "Crash guard is off: <reason>." with one of the reasons above (systemd, process 1, `xvfb-run` missing) or "no app data directory"; and "Crash guard did not start: <error>" when the launch target could not be worked out, or "Crash guard did not start (<error>); it is tried again shortly." when the guard could not be spawned (retried as in step 3 of [How it works](#how-it-works)).

When the guard is off there is no pipe, no report and no notice. When only spawning the guard failed, the run is still set up for one (`link::Connected::setup`): the panic hook is installed and writes its report, and a notice an earlier run left is still shown, but nothing relaunches after this run or leaves a notice unless a later retry starts a guard (step 3 of [How it works](#how-it-works)). A notice an earlier run of the same mode left in its state file is taken at startup in every case rather than kept forever (`take_notice`): a desktop run that is not set up for a guard drops it, a `serve` host prints its line. Old files are still removed, at startup and daily (see [Files](#files)). `crash_restart_enabled` and the Settings switch work as with a guard: they read and write `crash-restart.json`, which the guard of any run on this data directory reads when its app dies.

## Relaunch

- Desktop: the original args minus old `--crash-restart` marks, `--local-sign` and macOS `-psn_…`, plus the new mark. In a macOS `.app` bundle it runs `/usr/bin/open -n <bundle> --args …` through LaunchServices, so an updated executable is picked up; when the app's last focus line was `blur` it adds `-g`, so the relaunched window does not take focus from the app the user is in (`relaunch::open_args`). Otherwise it runs the executable directly in its own process group.
- Linux AppImage: the guard is spawned from, and relaunches, `$APPIMAGE` (the mount goes away with the app), but only when this executable really runs from that image (`relaunch::trusted_appimage`): its path is absolute with no `.` or `..` parts and lies under a `<temp>/.mount_*` directory, or under `$APPDIR` when `$APPDIR` itself looks like a mount point (`<temp>/.mount_*` or `/run/user/<uid>/<dir>`, never a broad prefix such as `/` or `/usr`), and `$APPIMAGE` is an absolute path to a file that exists. A PandaCode started from inside another AppImage inherits that app's variables and ignores them.
- `serve`: always restarts as a plain `serve --crash-restart=<ms>`. Sign-in, import and settings commands are one-shot. Its output goes to `serve-restart.log`. The relaunched host's environment has no `INVOCATION_ID` or `JOURNAL_STREAM` (`relaunch::relaunch_command`, `systemd::SERVICE_ENV`): it is the guard's child, not a systemd service, and must not take itself for one. Under `xvfb-run` it restarts as `xvfb-run -a <binary> serve …` without the old `DISPLAY` and `XAUTHORITY` too, because the old display died with the old `xvfb-run`. A desktop relaunch that runs the executable directly (Linux, Windows, a macOS executable outside a bundle) gets the guard's environment unchanged, which is the app's environment when it spawned that guard. A macOS bundle relaunch goes through `/usr/bin/open -n`, and LaunchServices starts the new process with the login session's environment, not the guard's: variables set only in the shell or by the tool that started PandaCode (such as `PANDACODE_CRASH_GUARD` or `PANDACODE_NO_CRASH_GUARD`) do not reach the relaunched app.
- A `serve` that starts with a pending notice in `crash-guard-serve.json` prints one line to stdout that matches what happened (`serve_notice`), with the crash time as a UTC clock time like a report header's (`clock_time`, "2026-11-12 14:32:05 UTC"): "PandaCode crashed at <time> and restarted. Details: <path>" after a relaunch; "PandaCode kept crashing, last at <time>, and didn't restart; automatic restarts resume by <time + 10 min>. Details: …" when restarts were paused, or "…; automatic restarts are on again. Details: …" once that time has passed (`now > crash time + 10 min`, the same instant the desktop notice uses); "PandaCode crashed at <time> and didn't restart: automatic restarts are off (turn them on in the desktop app's Settings, or in crash-restart.json in the data folder). Details: …" when the switch was off at the crash; "PandaCode crashed at <time> and didn't restart: the restart failed. Details: …" when starting the relaunch failed (also a relaunch that exited within 2 s with a status other than 0); and "PandaCode crashed at <time> and didn't restart. Details: …" otherwise (a handoff, a `serve` started by hand during the pause, a relaunch that died before taking the notice, a guard stopped during the pause, or a notice an older guard saved). `<path>` is the crash's own report, the notice's `report` file name (its last component only) under `crash-reports/`, when the notice names one, otherwise the `crash-reports/` folder; either is an absolute path, which names the user's home folder (on a relaunched host stdout is `serve-restart.log`). The reason comes from the notice's `notRestarted` (`off` or `failed`), which the guard sets (`state::NotRestarted`). It never prints the cause, since panic text can hold user content and stdout goes to logs.

## The notice

`showCrashRecovery` (`src/crash-recovery.ts`) shows at most one notice per page load: boot calls it once the tasks are listed and it has chosen the thread to open (before that thread has loaded), and it is called again when boot has finished or failed. A failing `crash_recovery_take` call is logged ("Could not check for a crash restart:") and counts as no crash; a malformed answer does too. Once a crash notice is on screen the page calls `crash_recovery_shown`, so the native side stops handing it out; a failure is logged ("Could not mark the crash notice as shown:") and the next page load gets the notice again. There are two notices: the crash notice, when the launch follows a crash, and the window notice, when it does not but the window's content died (see [The window notice](#the-window-notice)).

The crash notice's text comes from `crashNotice` (`src/crash-recovery-model.ts`), with `<when>` a clock time in the user's locale (`crashTime`): "at 14:32" for today, "on 3 Nov at 14:32" for another day, with the year when it is not this year. The sentences, in this order, each only when it applies:

- The lead. After an automatic restart (`restarted: true`): "PandaCode crashed <when> and restarted." After a crash that was not followed by a restart and restarts are not paused: "PandaCode crashed <when>." when the restarts-off line follows (the switch is off now), otherwise "PandaCode crashed <when> and didn't restart." (`notRestartedLead`: the relaunch failed, the user opened PandaCode during the guard's pause, the guard was stopped in that pause, or the switch was off at the crash and is on now or cannot be read). When restarts are paused: "PandaCode kept crashing, last <when>, and didn't restart." (`pausedLead`) while the whole notice fits, otherwise "PandaCode kept crashing and didn't restart." (`PAUSED_LEAD`).
- Only after a crash that was not restarted and restarts are not paused (after a restart the report holds the cause), "Cause: <cause>." for an OS crash (`plainCause`): for a signal a phrase with the name kept for searching, such as "a memory error (SIGSEGV)" (SIGSEGV and SIGBUS), "an invalid instruction (SIGILL)", "a math error (SIGFPE)", "a fatal error (SIGABRT)" (SIGABRT and SIGTRAP), "a blocked system call (SIGSYS)", or "an unexpected signal (…)" for another name (the guard keeps only those seven signal names in a notice, `policy::public_cause`, so this shows only for a notice from elsewhere; termination signals and SIGKILL are never a crash and have no phrase); or one of the Windows phrases (`an access violation`, `an illegal instruction`, `a stack overflow`, `a fatal error (abort)`, `heap corruption`). A panic gets no cause line. Anything else stays in the report.
- When the guard did not restart and restarts were not paused, "Automatic restarts are off in Settings." if `crash_restart_enabled` answers `false` (`RESTARTS_OFF_LINE`; nothing when it cannot be read). The notice then also has a **Crash settings** button (`showSettings`; see the actions below), also when the line itself gives way for length. When the switch is saved while the notice is up (`crashRestartChanged`, called by `src/crash-restart-settings.ts` after `set_crash_restart_enabled` succeeds), a notice whose setting could be read is written again, unannounced, with the new value: turned on, the restarts-off line and **Crash settings** go, the lead becomes "… and didn't restart." and the report line can follow; turned off, they come in. Behind another toast it is written afresh when it comes back. A restarted or paused notice, or one whose setting could not be read, is not affected.
- The agents line, whether or not restarts are paused, when agents may have been cut off (`interruptedByCrash`): when the native answer has `agentsInterrupted`, that flag alone decides (`commands::recovery`: true when this launch's database recovery marked at least one non-archived task `restart_interrupted`, or the held notice already said so, which it carries only from an earlier run that never showed that notice, never from a notice that was shown; see [Orphaned agents](#orphaned-agents)), so a task left Interrupted by an earlier quit never makes this crash claim it stopped agents. Only when the answer has no `agentsInterrupted` (never from this build's native side, which always sends it, false for a notice an older guard saved) does the task check `anyInterruptedByCrash` decide: a non-archived task with status `interrupted` active in the 24 hours before the crash. No count is claimed. Which line it is depends on the task list when the notice is first written (`watching` in `showCrashRecovery`: any non-archived Interrupted task, of any age). When the list holds one: "Running agents were stopped; their threads show as Interrupted." (`INTERRUPTED_LINE`), or "Stopped agents show as Interrupted." (`INTERRUPTED_SHORT`) when a held-back thread is named in the notice (it leaves the name room) or when the notice is too long; it goes once none is left (the last one resumed, archived or deleted): `main.ts` calls `tasksChanged` whenever the sidebar renders, and the notice is rewritten in place, unannounced, while it stays up. When the list holds none (the guard's answer alone, for example for tasks the list leaves out), there is nothing to point at or watch: it says "Running agents were stopped." (`AGENTS_STOPPED_LINE`), which is never shortened and stays.
- When boot held back a thread (see [What a launch restores](#what-a-launch-restores)) that still exists, is not archived and has not been opened since, "Your last thread, “<title>”, was kept closed in case it caused this." (`heldBackLine`; the title on one line, cut with "…" to 40 characters, `TITLE_LIMIT`). When boot reopened an unsent New task instead, so the held-back thread was not the user's last place (`notLast`, from `stuckNotLast`), it is "The thread “<title>” was kept closed in case it caused this.". An untitled thread is "An untitled thread was kept closed in case it caused this." in both cases. When boot also opened no other thread by itself (restarts paused, or the window died again), it is one sentence that starts with why the pane is empty (`NO_THREAD_OPENED`): "No thread was opened, in case your last thread, “<title>”, caused this.", or "No thread was opened, in case an untitled thread caused this." for an untitled thread. When boot opened no thread by itself and there is no such thread to name (nothing was remembered, or the thread is gone or archived): "No thread was opened, in case one caused this." (`NOTHING_OPENED_LINE`), except in a paused notice once the crash is more than 10 minutes old, where restarts are on again and it is left out. Once the user opens a thread or New task after boot opened none (`openThread` in `main.ts` clears `bootOpenedNothing` and calls `tasksChanged`, or `heldBackOpened` for the held-back thread), the notice is rewritten in place, unannounced: "No thread was opened, in case one caused this." goes, and a held-back sentence still shown takes its "Your last thread, “<title>”, was kept closed in case it caused this." form.
- Last, only when paused. "Automatic restarts resume …" and "Automatic restarts are on again." never give way for length; the report line after the latter does (see below). While the crash is at most 10 minutes old (`RESUME_WINDOW`, the guard's crash-loop window; past it restarts have resumed): "Automatic restarts resume <time>." (`pausedLine`), with `<time>` the first instant the guard no longer counts the crash (`resumesAt`: crash time + 10 minutes + 1 ms, since the guard's window includes its end) rounded up to a whole minute, so any crash at 14:32 (even 14:32:00.000) says "by 14:43" (or "by 3 Nov at 14:43" on another day; `resumeTime`). From `resumesAt` on: "Automatic restarts are on again." (`RESTARTS_BACK_LINE`), followed, when a report exists, by "If it crashes again, open a GitHub issue with the crash report (check it for private details first)." (`PAUSED_REPORT_LINE`).
- The next step after a crash that was not restarted and not paused, when a report exists and the restarts-off line is not shown (the switch is on, or could not be read): "If it keeps happening, open a GitHub issue with the crash report (check it for private details first)." (`REPORT_LINE`), at the very end.

The notice aims at 183 characters (`NOTICE_LIMIT`, counted in UTF-16 code units: the lowest limit that still holds, at the shortest title, what never gives way), a paused notice at 183 too (`PAUSED_NOTICE_LIMIT`: the lowest that holds its lead, the short agents line, the shortest held-back sentence at 30 characters of title and when restarts resume on another day of another year, en-US) and a paused notice past 10 minutes at 183 again (`RESUMED_NOTICE_LIMIT`); `tests/crash-recovery.test.mjs` checks every case. When it is longer, parts give way one after another in this order (`crashNotice`, `TRIMS`), each only while the notice still does not fit with the held-back title cut to the length given:

1. With the title at up to 40 characters, one step at a time: first, only in a paused notice past 10 minutes, the paused report line loses its parenthesis ("If it crashes again, open a GitHub issue with the crash report.", `PAUSED_REPORT_SHORT`) and then goes; then the long agents line takes its short form "Stopped agents show as Interrupted." (`INTERRUPTED_SHORT`; beside a held-back thread it is short from the start; "Running agents were stopped." stays as it is); then the not-restarted report line loses its parenthesis ("If it keeps happening, open a GitHub issue with the crash report.", `REPORT_SHORT`); then the paused lead loses its time (it becomes `PAUSED_LEAD`); then the next step after a crash that was not restarted goes (**Show crash report** still offers the report); then the cause line goes; then the held-back sentence drops "Your last thread," (or "The thread") and becomes "“<title>” was kept closed in case it caused this.", or, when boot opened no other thread, "No thread was opened, in case “<title>” caused this." (an untitled thread keeps "an untitled thread"); then, only when paused, the held-back sentence takes its shortest form (`keptClosedLine`): "“<title>” was kept closed." or "An untitled thread was kept closed.", and, when boot opened no other thread, "“<title>” was kept closed; no thread was opened." (or "An untitled thread was kept closed; no thread was opened.") in place of the "No thread was opened, in case …" form, since the paused lead already says it did not restart.
2. With the title at up to 30 characters (`MIN_TITLE`): the restarts-off line goes; then, last and only in that paused shortest form, "; no thread was opened" goes ("“<title>” was kept closed."). "Automatic restarts resume …" never does.

Then parts come back, in this order (a line that was dropped, not shortened, never does):

3. If the agents line was shortened for length and the notice fits with the whole line again (title at up to 40 characters), it comes back whole.
4. If the paused held-back sentence took its shortest form and the notice fits with the longer one again (title at up to 40 characters), that comes back; otherwise, if "Your last thread," (or "your last thread,", or "The thread") was dropped and the notice fits with it again, it comes back.
5. The title is cut, from 40 characters down to no fewer than 30, until the notice fits.
6. If a report line that is still there lost its parenthesis, "(check it for private details first)" comes back when the notice still fits with it at the title length step 5 settled on (the paused report line first, then the not-restarted one; the two never apply together).
7. When paused and the lead lost its time, the time comes back ("PandaCode kept crashing, last <when>, and didn't restart.") if the notice still fits with it at that title length.

The lead, the thread's name, that no thread was opened (the nothing-opened line, or, unless paused, the start of the held-back sentence), "Automatic restarts resume …" and "Automatic restarts are on again." never give way, and the agents line is only shortened, never dropped for length, so a notice can still pass its limit.

The clock time stays true however long the notice waits. The text is written again each time the notice is shown, and while it is showing it is rewritten in place (`keepUntilClosed`, unannounced): when the date changes, so "at 14:32" becomes "on 3 Nov at 14:32" (the resume time too) (checked just after the next local midnight and at least every hour, since a sleeping computer stops timers), when the Settings switch is saved (see the restarts-off line above), and, when restarts are paused, once the crash is more than 10 minutes old (`changesAt`), so "Automatic restarts resume by 14:43." (and an unnamed "No thread was opened, in case one caused this.") gives way to "Automatic restarts are on again. If it crashes again, open a GitHub issue with the crash report (check it for private details first)." (the report line shortened or dropped when the notice is too long) or, without a report, to "Automatic restarts are on again."; once no Interrupted task is left, without the agents sentence; and once a thread is opened after boot opened none, without the nothing-opened wording (see above).

### The window notice

Without a crash notice, when the window's content died before this page (`reloadNotice`; see [What a launch restores](#what-a-launch-restores) for how that is known), the toast starts with one of:

- "PandaCode's window stopped and was reloaded." (`WINDOW_RELOADED_LINE`): this page is the reload after the window's content died.
- "PandaCode's window stopped again and was reloaded." (`WINDOW_RELOADED_AGAIN_LINE`): it died again before the reloaded page had lived 90 s, and this page is another reload.
- "Last time PandaCode ran, its window failed." (`WINDOW_STOPPED_BEFORE_LINE`): it died again and this is a new launch, not a reload: usually after the native dialog's **Restart PandaCode**, but also after a quit and reopen by hand, so it does not claim a restart.

It is followed by the held-back sentence when boot held a thread back, in the same wordings as in the crash notice (in its "No thread was opened, in case your last thread, “<title>”, caused this." form when boot opened none by itself, which is always so after "again" and "before" unless a New task page was reopened, and then "The thread “<title>” was kept closed in case it caused this."), or by "No thread was opened, in case one caused this." when boot opened no thread and names none. It has no time, no cause and no **Show crash report**, and nothing in it is trimmed.

A single window death that held nothing back shows no toast: on the first reload ("reloaded") with no thread held back and a thread or New task opened as usual, everything came back, so `showCrashRecovery` only logs "PandaCode's window stopped and was reloaded." to the console (`console.info`).

### How it behaves

- It stays until the user closes it: there is no timer. Opening the held-back thread, by **Open thread** or any other way (the sidebar or a tray item, for example: `openThread` in `main.ts` calls `heldBackOpened`), ends it, and hides it if it is showing, only when nothing else would be left in it (`remains` in `showCrashRecovery`): no **Show crash report**, not paused, no restarts-off line and no agents line. A window notice always ends. Otherwise the notice stays and is rewritten, unannounced, without the held-back sentence and without **Open thread**: at once when it is showing, or when it comes back after another toast (see the actions below).
- A held-back thread that is deleted or archived while the notice waits is no longer offered: the next time the notice is written (when it comes back after another toast, when its time is rewritten, or when **Open thread** is pressed for it, which then only rewrites the notice) it loses the held-back sentence and **Open thread**, and when boot opened no thread by itself "No thread was opened, in case one caused this." takes the sentence's place. A notice left with no button keeps its close button.
- When another toast takes the one toast slot, the notice comes back once that toast has gone, as often as that happens (`keepUntilClosed`), without its entrance animation.
- It is announced to screen readers once: when it comes back after another toast, or when its time is rewritten, its region is set to `aria-live="off"`.
- It never covers panel content the user works in: the composer, the inspector or the action dock. It sits in the conversation pane: its right edge 16 px inside the pane (8 px left of an inspector drawn over the pane, or of the dock when a tall notice would reach it) and its bottom 8 px above the composer, or 16 px above the pane's bottom when there is no composer (`placeToast` in `src/toast-clearance.ts` sets `--toast-right`, `--toast-lift` and `--toast-max`, read by `.toast.toast-dismissible` in `design-system.css`). It may cover the transcript, which then gets as much room at its end while the notice shows (`makeRoom` sets `--toast-room` on the transcript, added to the end spacer `#transcript::after` in `turn-card.css`), so the last message can scroll clear of the notice; a transcript that was scrolled to its bottom stays there. When an inspector is drawn over the pane (a narrow window) and less than 180 px (`MIN_WIDTH`) is left between the pane's left edge (plus 8 px) and the drawer, the notice never covers the drawer: it is hidden (`data-crowded` on the toast, `visibility: hidden` in `design-system.css`) and the transcript gets no end room. It shows again when there is room or the drawer closes, unannounced when its text has not changed since it was last on screen. In the board and GitHub views (no composer) it sits 16 px inside the pane's bottom right, and the view's scroller (`#project-board .board-columns`, `#github-hub .gh-body`) gets the end room instead of the transcript, so its last cards or rows can scroll clear. Settings covers the whole workspace and has controls down its right side, so while Settings is open the notice is hidden (`visibility: hidden` on `.toast.toast-dismissible` after a `.workspace` holding a visible `#settings-page`, in `design-system.css`) and shows again, unchanged, when Settings closes. The place is applied at once, never animated, and rechecked whenever the toast, the composer, the panes, the inspector, the dock or the Settings, board and GitHub views change size, when those views draw their content, or when the window resizes (`keepToastClear`). Ordinary toasts keep their corner, since they leave by themselves. A dismissible toast also shows its whole text (no line clamp).
- It has the same close button as every toast, labelled "Dismiss" for screen readers (`DISMISS_LABEL`), with no tooltip. It names no key, because Escape closes the notice only while focus is inside the toast; anywhere else Escape belongs to the page (it stops a running agent). While focus is in the toast the close button carries `aria-keyshortcuts="Escape"`, and an Escape pressed there never reaches the page. F6 moves focus to the toast's first button and F6 again moves it back (to the composer when there is no earlier place to return to, for example after clicking into the notice); that first button announces F6 in `aria-keyshortcuts` (`src/toast-keys.ts`). Once a button in the toast has done its work, focus goes back to where it was. While a modal dialog makes the toast inert, F6 does nothing: focus stays put and the key is not consumed.
- Its actions: when a thread was held back, **Open thread** (`OPEN_THREAD`; its accessible name is "Open thread “<full title>”", `openThreadName`) opens that thread; when a report exists (crash notice only), **Show crash report** (`crash_report_reveal` shows this notice's own report in the file manager, as named in the notice's `report`, while that file still exists and is not dated more than a day after now (`FUTURE_SLACK_MS`, `report::named`); when it is gone or dated that far ahead, or the notice came from an older guard without a report name, the newest panic or crash report, which may be another mode's since desktop and `serve` share `crash-reports/`; the webview never sees a path or a file name). When restarts are off (crash notice only; `showSettings`), **Crash settings** (`CRASH_SETTINGS`; accessible name "Crash settings, in Settings › General › Crash recovery") opens Settings → General at the Crash recovery group (`showCrashSettings` in `main.ts`); the notice stays and, hidden while Settings is open, shows again after. The buttons come in this order: **Open thread**, **Show crash report**, **Crash settings**. Only **Open thread** is ever the main (accented) button; **Show crash report** and **Crash settings** are always quieter ones (`quiet`), also when they come first.
- What each action leaves: the close button (or Escape with focus in the toast) ends it for good. **Open thread** opens the thread; when nothing else would be left (as above) that ends the notice for good, otherwise the notice is shown again at once, unannounced, without the held-back sentence (and without the nothing-opened line, since a thread is open now) and without **Open thread**, keeping its other sentences and buttons. **Show crash report** and **Crash settings** leave the notice as it is; if revealing fails, "Could not show the crash report: …" takes the toast for 6 s and the notice comes back after it.

## What a launch restores

Any launch, crash or not, restores composer drafts (unless **Keep unsent drafts** is off) and the thread that was open (`src/launch-restore.ts`, local storage only).

Local storage is the webview's, not the app data directory. No window sets a data directory (`tauri.conf.json`, `lib.rs`), so Tauri 2.12's defaults apply. On Windows Tauri points WebView2 at the app *local* data directory, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ai.pandacode.desktop\` (WebView2 keeps its data in `EBWebView\` there), which is outside `%APPDATA%\ai.pandacode.desktop\`. On Linux it points WebKitGTK at the same app local data directory, which there is the app data directory (`$XDG_DATA_HOME` or `~/.local/share/ai.pandacode.desktop/`). On macOS Tauri sets nothing and WKWebView uses its default store for the app, normally under `~/Library/WebKit/ai.pandacode.desktop/`; this path comes from WebKit's usual behaviour and is not set or checked by PandaCode or Tauri.

Privacy: unsent drafts, which can include a pasted secret, stay there as plain text for up to 30 days (see Drafts below), outside the app data folder on macOS and Windows, so deleting that folder does not remove them. Settings → General → Crash recovery → **Keep unsent drafts** turned off stops keeping them and removes the ones kept so far; **Clear saved drafts** removes the ones kept so far without changing the switch (see Keeping off and Clearing below). Who can read them: on Linux PandaCode makes that folder owner-only (0700) at every desktop or `serve` start (`files::narrow_webview_data`, see [Files](#files)), so only your user (and root) can; a symlink at the folder is left as it is. On macOS and Windows PandaCode narrows nothing: the permissions are whatever the webview gives its store and the parent folders give (on Windows the profile's `%LOCALAPPDATA%`).

The keys:

| Key | Holds | Removed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pandacode.draft:<task id>` | `{"t": savedAt, "text": …}`, a thread's unsent text | When sent, cleared, expired or pushed out by the size budget; when its thread is deleted (at once while PandaCode is open, otherwise at the next launch) |
| `pandacode.draft:new:<project id>` | The same, for the project's New task (`NEW_TASK_DRAFT`) | When sent, emptied, expired or pushed out, or when this window removes the project; at the next launch when the project no longer exists; when the New task that owns it is no longer a draft (its text moves to `pandacode.draft:<task id>`) |
| `pandacode.draft:channel:<channel id>` | The same, for a shared bot channel's composer (`CHANNEL_DRAFT`, `channelDraftKey`) | When posted (removed before the post; a failed post puts the text back unless something new was typed there), emptied, expired or pushed out; at the first launch whose `bot_shared_channels` list loads without that channel (archived channels are listed and keep theirs) |
| `pandacode.keepDrafts` | `off` while Settings' **Keep unsent drafts** is off (`KEEP_DRAFTS_KEY`) | When the switch is turned on again |
| `pandacode.openThread` | The id of the open thread | When nothing is selected; when boot keeps a thread closed or opens none (but not while it reopens an unsent New task) |
| `pandacode.openNewTask` | `1` while the open thread is a New task | With `pandacode.openThread` |
| `pandacode.restoringThread` | The id of the thread being opened, by boot or by the user (the latest open replaces it) | When that thread has painted, 5 s after it rendered, or when the document becomes hidden, whichever comes first; when it failed to open or a later open has taken its place; on `pagehide` and on the native `app-quitting` event; and at the next boot, after reading it |
| `pandacode.restoringPrevious` | The thread remembered as open (`pandacode.openThread`) when the marked thread began to open, only when that is another thread (`beginThreadRestore`) | With the mark it belongs to (painted, 5 s, hidden, failed, page going away: `finishThreadRestore`); replaced or removed when the next thread opens (removed when no thread, or that same thread, is remembered then); at the next boot, after reading it |
| `pandacode.pageAlive` | `1` from page load | On `pagehide` and on `app-quitting` |
| `pandacode.windowDied` | Written by the first reload after the window's content died: the marked thread, else the remembered thread (none for a New task), else an empty string | After the page has lived 90 s, on `pagehide` and on `app-quitting`, and by the next launch that is not a reload |

- Drafts: every change is written to local storage at once (`PersistedDrafts.set`, `write`), with no delay to wait out. The webview may write local storage to disk slightly later, so the very last keystrokes before a hard crash can be missing; this has not been tested in a real crash. Sending or clearing a draft removes it at once, so a crash right after a send never brings the prompt back; text that is empty or only whitespace is removed, not stored. What a crash can still lose: a draft over 50,000 UTF-16 code units (`MAX_DRAFT_CHARS`), which is not stored and whose stored copy is removed once it grows past that (it stays in memory for that launch only); a draft the size budget pushed out of storage or a write that failed even after making room ("Could not keep a composer draft:"), both kept in memory only; attachments, which are never stored; and whatever the webview had not yet written to disk (see [Limits](#limits)). A draft is dropped only when it is older than 30 days (`DRAFT_MAX_AGE`, counted from its last change) or dated more than a day in the future (`DRAFT_FUTURE_SLACK`: a clock that ran ahead, which would otherwise keep it past 30 days), malformed, too large, pushed out by the size budget, or its thread is deleted (see Deleting below). A draft is never dropped merely because its task is missing from the task list, which holds at most 500 tasks and leaves out architect worker threads. Expiry runs at launch, then once a day while the app runs and whenever the window becomes visible again (`expireDraftsWhileRunning`, `EXPIRE_EVERY`), so a draft outlives 30 days by at most a day while PandaCode runs, and by however long PandaCode is not opened. The stored total stays under about 1 MB (`DRAFTS_BYTE_BUDGET`), dropping the oldest first; a full store drops the older half and retries once. Dropped drafts stay in memory for the running launch.
- Text the app puts in the composer is saved like typing, since those paths either raise the composer's `input` event or write the draft themselves (`state.drafts.set` in `main.ts`): a suggestion placed in the composer (the `suggest` action, `fillComposer`), text inserted from the /btw panel or a dialog, a picked slash command or file mention, a rejected message put back in the composer (`restoreComposerDraft`), the draft of a fork, and the typed text a newly created task takes over from the task it was created from. A fill that is sent at once (a deploy brief) is never a draft. Redrawing the composer also saves what it holds at that moment.
- New task text: a New task is a native draft session that the next launch discards while it has no messages, so its text is kept under one key per project instead, `pandacode.draft:new:<project id>`, newest only: the New task typed in last owns the key, and one that owned it before keeps its text in memory only. The next New task opened in that project takes the text over; sending it or emptying the composer removes it, so sent or deleted text never comes back. Once the task is no longer a draft (its first message was sent, also by a delivered queued message: `reclaim`), the project key is removed and any text the task still holds is written under its own key, `pandacode.draft:<task id>`, like any thread's (`claim` in `PersistedDrafts`); a New task moved to another project moves its text to that project's key the same way. When a New task's first send fails it is marked a New task again (`sendDraft` in `main.ts` sets `draft` and calls `reclaim`), so the text put back in the composer is kept under the project key again rather than under the task's own id, which the next launch would discard with the empty draft session; `get_task` then says whether native storage saved it after all, and `reclaim` runs again, as it does for every thread draft after each task list load (`reclaimAll` in `loadSessions`). The same age, size and budget rules apply.
- Shared channel text: a shared bot channel's composer keeps its text under `pandacode.draft:channel:<channel id>`, written on every `input` and before the channel view is refreshed, with the same age, size and budget rules as other drafts. Posting removes it at once; a post that fails puts the text back unless something new was typed there meanwhile. Once per page load, after `bot_shared_channels` has answered (never after a failed load), the text of channels missing from that list is dropped (`dropRemovedChannelDrafts`); archived channels are in the list and keep theirs. These keys are never sent to `sessions_missing`.
- Clearing: Settings → General → Crash recovery → **Clear saved drafts** (`clearSavedDrafts` in `main.ts`, `PersistedDrafts.clearStored`) removes every stored `pandacode.draft:` key at once, with no confirmation: thread, New task and shared channel text, also keys this launch never loaded. It drops them from memory too, except text showing in an open composer (the thread's or New task's message box, and a shared channel's): that stays on screen and in memory, unstored until its next change, which stores it again. It then shows the toast "Saved drafts cleared." It leaves everything else in local storage (the open thread and restore marks) and attachments, which are never stored. It does not stop drafts being kept: the next change to any composer stores it again (Keep unsent drafts off does).
- Keeping off: with **Keep unsent drafts** off (`pandacode.keepDrafts` = `off`, `PersistedDrafts.setKeeping`) no draft is written. Turning it off removes every stored `pandacode.draft:` key at once, but all text in memory stays, so open composers keep their text for the running launch. Every launch while it is off removes any stored draft key and restores none (the `PersistedDrafts` constructor). The open thread and the restore marks are unaffected. Turning it on again stores each draft from its next change.
- Deleting: a thread deleted while PandaCode is open (in this window, or anywhere that sends the `sessions-deleted` event) loses its draft at once (`removeDeletedSessions`). A thread deleted while PandaCode was closed loses it at the next launch: once the tasks are listed, boot sends the thread ids that have a stored draft (not New task text) to `sessions_missing` in batches of at most 2,000 ids, leaving out ids over 128 characters (`dropDeletedDrafts`, `draftThreadBatches`), and drops the drafts of the ids that have no task row at all; archived threads, drafts and architect workers have one and keep theirs. A failed or malformed answer is logged ("Could not check for drafts of deleted threads: …") and the remaining batches are skipped for that launch. Removing a project in this window (`removeProject`) removes at once the drafts of its listed threads and its New task text (`remove_project` returns nothing, so threads missing from the list, such as architect workers or threads beyond the 500 listed, are not named); the database deletes those threads with the project, so their drafts go at the next launch through `sessions_missing`. A project removed any other way (another window, a remote client) loses its New task text at the next launch: once `list_projects` has answered (not after a failed load), boot drops the New task text of every project id missing from the list (`dropRemovedProjectDrafts`).
- Open thread: `pandacode.openThread` is set once a thread's messages have rendered and cleared when nothing is selected (for example after choosing a project with no threads, so the next launch lands in that project). When the open thread was a New task, `pandacode.openNewTask` is set too, and boot reopens the saved project's New task page (with its kept text) instead of a thread; a New task that was sent since reopens as the ordinary thread it became. An unsent New task has no transcript to suspect, so its page and text come back in every case below too, also when restarts are paused or the window died again; it stays remembered and is never named as the held-back thread. Its project is the saved project (`pandacode.project`): when that project no longer exists (removed elsewhere since), no New task page opens and boot falls back as on any launch, to the newest thread of the project it then activates (the newest listed thread's project), or, when restarts are paused or the window died again, to no thread at all (with the nothing-opened wording in the notice). At boot the remembered thread reopens, with its project, unless it is archived or gone. Otherwise boot opens the saved project's newest thread.
- The restoring mark: every thread opened, by boot or by the user (`openThread` in `main.ts`, which `selectSession` calls), is first written to `pandacode.restoringThread` (`beginThreadRestore`); there is one key, so a later open replaces an earlier one's mark. The mark is cleared once that thread's messages have rendered and been painted (two animation frames after the render, `finishThreadRestoreAfterPaint`, which leaves a later thread's mark alone; so a transcript that takes the app down while painting stays marked). A window that paints nothing (covered, minimised, hidden) runs no animation frames, so the mark also goes 5 s after the render (`PAINT_WAIT`) or as soon as the document becomes hidden, whichever comes first. It is cleared at once when its opening fails or a later open superseded it before it rendered (`finishThreadRestore`), and, whatever thread it names, when the page goes away in an orderly way: on `pagehide`, or on the native `app-quitting` event, which `on_run_event` sends to the main webview once a deliberate exit (Quit, an update restart, the dialog's **Restart PandaCode**) is committed, since WKWebView may not fire `pagehide` on Cmd+Q (`leavePage`, which also removes `pandacode.pageAlive` and `pandacode.windowDied`; drafts need nothing there, since each change is already written). When boot opens no thread it writes no mark. The next boot always removes the mark after reading it.
- A thread that takes the app down: a mark still there at the next boot counts only when that boot follows a crash (a crash notice) or a window death. After a crash, opening the marked thread (whether boot or the user opened it) may have taken the app down, so boot does not open it, not even as the newest-thread fallback, and forgets `pandacode.openThread` and `pandacode.openNewTask`. It opens the thread that was open before the marked one (`pandacode.restoringPrevious`) when that is still listed and not archived, and otherwise the project's newest other thread. Otherwise (an exit that reached neither `pagehide` nor `app-quitting` while a thread was opening, such as a silent kill) the mark is just cleared.
- When restarts are paused (`restartsPaused` in the notice, which boot awaits before choosing a thread), boot opens no thread by itself, not even the newest one, since any thread may be the one that crashes, and forgets the mark and (unless an unsent New task was open, whose page reopens) `pandacode.openThread` and `pandacode.openNewTask`. The suspect it names is the marked thread, or when there is no mark (the crash came after the thread loaded) the remembered thread (none when a New task was open) (`threadToReopen`).
- A window whose content died: a page knows it reloads a dead window when its navigation type is `reload` and `pandacode.pageAlive`, written at every page load and removed on `pagehide`, is still there, so the last page never reached `pagehide` (`windowContentDied`). A single death is usually harmless (often macOS ending the content process under memory pressure), so this first reload holds back only a thread that was mid-open: the marked thread, which it keeps closed, forgetting `pandacode.openThread` and `pandacode.openNewTask`, and opening the thread open before it (`pandacode.restoringPrevious`, when still listed and not archived) or else the project's newest other thread. With no mark (the content died after the thread loaded) nothing is held back and the remembered thread (or New task) reopens as usual. Either way it notes a suspect in `pandacode.windowDied`: the marked thread, else the remembered thread (none when a New task was open), else an empty string. The note is removed once the page has lived 90 s (`WINDOW_STABLE_AFTER`, longer than the 60 s native reload budget) or goes away in an orderly way (`pagehide` or `app-quitting`).
- A window that died again: a boot that finds `pandacode.windowDied` knows the window died before it proved stable. It opens no thread by itself, like paused restarts (an unsent New task's page still reopens), forgets `pandacode.openThread` and `pandacode.openNewTask` (except for that New task), and holds back the noted thread (or, when the note is empty, the mark or else the remembered thread). The remembered thread is held back only here and when restarts are paused, and only when there is neither a note nor a mark. When this boot is itself another reload of a dead window, the note stays. When it is a new launch, which is what follows the native dialog's **Restart PandaCode** (the dead page could not run `pagehide` or take `app-quitting`, so its note survived the restart), the note is honoured this once and removed.
- When the held-back thread still exists, is not archived and has not been opened since, the notice names it and offers **Open thread**, also when it has a **Show crash report** button (see [The notice](#the-notice)).
- Attachments are not restored. Files added to the composer live only in the running page's state (`state.attachments` in `main.ts`, never stored), so after a crash or any other exit the draft's text comes back without them.

## Limits

- The macOS system "quit unexpectedly" dialog: after a panic it is not expected, since a release build ends with `_exit(101)` once the guard has both lines (see the panic hook above); if a line was lost the abort follows and the dialog can appear. After a real fatal signal (a crash in native code, or `kill -SEGV`) it is still expected beside the relaunched window, and its Reopen button only brings the running window forward. Neither has been checked on a release build.
- The Windows code (job breakaway, NTSTATUS exit codes, the writer thread, `Process::gone`/`wait_gone` on `OpenProcess` handles, `FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT` reads, writes, log and lock opens with their regular-file check) has never been run on Windows. During development the crash-guard modules were type-checked and passed clippy for `x86_64-pc-windows-msvc` and `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu` in a scratch crate (with shims for the Tauri types). CI now runs clippy on the whole crate for Windows (x64 and arm64) and Linux on every push to `main` (`rust-platforms` in `ci.yml`), so the code compiles there; it still has not been run. The native tests that run a real guard (`crash_guard/tests.rs`) are Unix only.
- On Windows `serve-restart.log` is capped only when a relaunch opens it: a relaunched host that runs for months can grow it past 1 MB until the next crash.
- Only macOS relaunches in the background: a bundle relaunch uses `open -g` when PandaCode was not in front. On Linux and Windows the relaunched window may take focus from the app the user is in.
- "2 startup crashes in a row" counts crash records only. A run that ended normally (a quit, an update, a forced quit) leaves no record, so it does not break the run: two startup crashes within 10 minutes pause restarts even with a healthy run between them. A crash while the switch was off leaves no record either.
- Attachments in the composer are not restored after a crash; only the draft's text is. A draft over 50,000 UTF-16 code units (`MAX_DRAFT_CHARS`; emoji and other characters outside the Basic Multilingual Plane count as two) is not stored, and neither is one the size budget pushed out or whose write failed.
- Linux-only behaviour (AppImage relaunch and `$APPDIR` trust, `/proc` start times, the systemd check, the `xvfb-run` re-wrap, data directories) is unit tested in parts but has never run on Linux.
- Kills by the system for memory (the Linux OOM killer, macOS jetsam) and Force Quit are SIGKILLs: nothing reaches the pipe, so they are treated as silent kills and never relaunched, by design.
- Hangs are not detected. A PandaCode that stops responding but stays alive is never restarted; only its death is noticed.
- When a webview's content process dies, only macOS reloads the window (`webview.rs`). On Linux (WebKitGTK) and Windows (WebView2) the app keeps running with a blank window and the guard does nothing, since the app did not die.
- A window death is held against a thread only for 90 s (`WINDOW_STABLE_AFTER`), and a first reload holds back only a thread that was mid-open: a thread that kills the window's content after it has painted is reopened by the first reload, and one that does so more than 90 s after each reload is reopened every time. The native budget counts only reloads within 60 s, so such a window is reloaded again and again.
- The window-death note outlives any exit that reaches neither `pagehide` nor `app-quitting`: when PandaCode crashes, is killed or ends from a termination signal within 90 s of a window reload, the next launch reads the note as the window having died again (a crash notice after a crash, otherwise "Last time PandaCode ran, its window failed."), opens no thread by itself and holds back the noted thread, although no thread may have been at fault.
- `app-quitting` is sent without waiting for the page (`emit_to`, never awaited), so on a quit the page may be gone before it handles the event; if WKWebView also skips `pagehide`, the restoring mark stays (the next boot, which follows no crash, just clears it).
- On macOS the Dock's Quit, a logout and any other `terminate:` still bypass `ExitRequested` (only the app menu's Quit and Cmd+Q were rerouted, `app_menu.rs`): such a quit is announced to the guard at `RunEvent::Exit`, but `app-quitting` comes as the run loop ends, when the page is least likely to handle it, and running agents are ended at once rather than given 12 s to stop.
- The guard's check of its own arguments (step 1 of [How it works](#how-it-works)) is not a security boundary against a process of the same user: such a process controls the environment (`$HOME`, `$XDG_DATA_HOME`, `$APPIMAGE`, `$APPDIR`, `XAUTHORITY`) the guard derives its relaunch target, data folder and wrapper from, so it can make them agree with any arguments it passes.
- The guard's command line (`pandacode __crash-guard <pid> <data dir> <target> <mode> <launched at> armed|disarmed <wrapper or -> -- <app args>`) holds the data folder's path, which names the user's home folder, and the app's own arguments. Other local users can read it in the process list for as long as the guard runs, which is at least as long as the app.
- Symlinks are refused only at the last path part: crash files are opened with `O_NOFOLLOW`, and `crash-reports/` and the Linux webview data folder are refused when they themselves are a symlink (`files::private_dir`). A symlink at a parent folder (the data folder or a folder above it) is followed, so whoever can replace such a folder with a link can redirect where crash files are written.
- A thread deleted while PandaCode is closed keeps its draft in local storage until the next launch, and longer when that launch's `sessions_missing` check fails. A project removed outside this window keeps its New task text in local storage until the next launch whose project list loads.
- `xvfb-run` cases the code does not handle: it detects `xvfb-run` only by `XAUTHORITY` pointing into an `xvfb-run.` temp directory, so a `serve` under `xvfb-run -f <file>` (its own auth file) or a hand-started `Xvfb` is relaunched plainly, with the old `DISPLAY`, and finds the old display gone. A relaunched `serve` starts its guard only after the app is built, so a death while building it is seen by no guard: there is one relaunch and then nothing, and the startup-crash limit never comes into it. When that relaunched process has exited within 2 s, the guard counts the relaunch as failed (see step 5 of [What the guard does after a crash](#what-the-guard-does-after-a-crash)): it logs "Crash guard could not relaunch PandaCode: the relaunched app exited at once (<status>)" in `crash-guard.log` (or, for exit status 0, "Crash guard: the relaunched app handed off to a running PandaCode.", although a `serve` hands off to nothing) and puts the `restarted: false` notice back, so the next `serve` started by hand prints "PandaCode crashed at <time> and didn't restart. Details: …". When it dies later than 2 s but before its own `start`, the notice was never taken by the relaunch, so the next `serve` started by hand prints that line too. The re-wrap is always `xvfb-run -a`, so the original `xvfb-run` options (such as `-s` screen settings or `-n`) are not kept. Only `serve` is re-wrapped: a desktop app under `xvfb-run` relaunches without it.
- A systemd service the check does not recognise gets a guard beside systemd's own `Restart=`: one that runs `serve` through a wrapper (a shell, `xvfb-run`) with its output sent away from the journal, so neither `JOURNAL_STREAM` nor the parent's name gives it away (`systemd.rs`). systemd's default `KillMode` then ends the guard with the rest of the unit when the host dies. The parent's name is read on Linux only.
- A crash before the guard starts is not guarded: the desktop app starts it first in `.setup`, after plugin setup, so a crash during plugin setup (or earlier) is neither relaunched nor reported by the guard. `serve` starts it after the app is built, so a crash while building the app is not guarded either; its setup and the running host are. A setup that fails with an error (not a crash) is a deliberate exit in both modes and is never relaunched, so a fault that a restart would fix (a full disk freed meanwhile) needs the user to start PandaCode again.
- PandaCode running as process 1 (a container without an init) runs no guard, since nothing outlives it.
- A crash whose state the guard cannot save (the state file's lock not had within 2 s, a full disk, no write access) is neither relaunched nor announced: the guard starts nothing, since without a saved history the loop limit could not stop a crash loop, and no notice is left for the next launch. Only `crash-guard.log` records it ("Crash guard could not record the crash, so PandaCode was not restarted.").
- On macOS and Linux only a death announced on the pipe counts as a crash. A crash so severe that the signal handler cannot run is treated as a silent kill and is not relaunched. One such case is a stack overflow on a thread that has no alternate signal stack: the handlers run on the alternate stack (`SA_ONSTACK`), which Rust std sets up only for the main thread and threads it starts, so a thread it did not start (such as one of WebKit's) that overflows its stack dies without writing `signal N`. That death is treated as a kill: no relaunch, no report from the guard and no notice (see [Crash or deliberate exit](#crash-or-deliberate-exit)).
- On Windows the panic lines go through the link (skipped when its lock is taken) and the writer thread's queue, so they can be lost to the abort that follows; the NTSTATUS exit code still marks the crash.
- A leaderless agent group can be mistaken (`process::group_is_ours`). When a group's leader is gone, the guard kills the group if one of its members started between the registered leader start and the app's death, which does not prove the members are the agent's. If the agent's whole group ended while the guard still listed it (its `unchild` was dropped and no resync followed, or the app died first), the kernel could give the leader's pid to an unrelated process that became a group leader itself, started children before the app died and then exited: that group has the same id, no leader and members inside the range, and is killed. It needs a pid reuse plus those steps inside one app run. The range itself is not otherwise bounded, and on macOS it is in wall-clock time (start times and the death time are microseconds since the epoch), so a clock change during the run moves it: a clock set back between the registration and the death empties it (the leaderless group is not stopped), one set forward widens it. On Linux both are clock ticks since boot, which no clock change moves. Telling the two apart needs an identity the guard does not have (a per-member record from the app, or a pidfd or kqueue watch held from registration).
- The guard tracks at most 4096 agent groups (`MAX_CHILDREN`); groups beyond that are not stopped after a crash.
- Any process holding the pipe's write end (on macOS an agent subprocess can inherit it in the close-on-exec race of step 4 of [How it works](#how-it-works)) can send `child` lines naming process groups of the same user, which the guard then stops when the app dies. This gives no extra privilege: such a process can already signal those groups itself.
- Local VMs PandaCode started are shut down only by the normal exit path (`vm::Vms::shutdown`). After a crash they keep running, and the guard does not stop them. Cloud sandboxes are also left as they are.
- The setting is read from `crash-restart.json` when the app dies. Only when that file is missing or cannot be read does the guard fall back to the last `arm`/`disarm` line it received, which can be out of date while a dropped line has not been resent yet (the next send or the 15 s check resends it, unless the pipe is still full).
- Old reports, logs, crash times, notices and temp files are removed only by a running desktop app or `serve` host (at startup and daily). While PandaCode is not running they stay, however old. A relaunched `serve` host never removes its own `serve-restart.log` (its stdout), however long it has been unwritten; another process's prune does.
- On Windows a prune can remove a log another running process still writes to: there is no refresh of a log in use, no reopening and no live rotation there, so a quiet `serve` host or a long-lived guard can lose its open log to another process's prune after 90 days without a write. On Unix a log in use keeps a fresh modification time and is not pruned; if it is deleted by hand, a relaunched `serve` host opens it again within 30 s, while the guard re-points its stderr at `crash-guard.log` only once the app has died, so a line it logs earlier in its watch can go into the deleted file.
- When a start cannot clear the pending notice (the state file cannot be saved), it removes the state file, losing the crash history, so the loop limit starts counting again. When it cannot remove it either (no write access to the data folder), the notice is never shown, and the process keeps the state file's lock for its whole run, so a guard pausing before a relaunch starts nothing and every other process of that mode on this data directory (its guards included) fails to save after waiting 2 s, until that process exits. The process's own later saves to that file (the daily prune) fail at once, without the 2 s wait, and are logged.
- A start that cannot get the state file's lock within 2 s takes no notice; it stays for a later launch.
- In a window that paints nothing (covered or minimised), the restoring mark goes 5 s after the thread's messages rendered or when the document becomes hidden, so a transcript that takes the app down when the window is next shown is not held back.
- A crash notice the page never showed is written back at a deliberate exit only when the state file's lock can be had within 2 s and the save succeeds; otherwise it is lost (logged). A desktop app that dies before its page showed the notice does not write it back itself. After a termination signal (SIGTERM, SIGINT, SIGHUP, SIGQUIT) or a silent kill the guard writes it back from its `held` line, so it survives, unless the guard is gone too, its `held` line was lost and not yet resent, or the write-back fails (logged). After another crash the new notice replaces it, keeping only that agents were stopped.
- A pending notice of a mode that is never started again is removed after 90 days (or once dated more than a day ahead) only by a prune, which needs a running desktop app or `serve` host.
- Drafts and the restoring marks live in the webview's local storage, which the webview may write to disk a moment after the page sets them. A native crash within moments of typing or of opening a thread may then lose the last keystrokes, or the mark that would hold that thread back. This has not been tested in a real crash.
- A macOS bundle relaunch runs with the login session's environment (LaunchServices), so variables set only in the shell that started PandaCode, such as `PANDACODE_CRASH_GUARD`, do not reach the relaunched app (see [Relaunch](#relaunch)).
- On Linux a desktop app whose setup fails shows no dialog of its own (see step 1 of [How it works](#how-it-works)): it starts `zenity`, `kdialog` or `notify-send` when one is installed in `/usr/bin` or `/bin` (one installed elsewhere, such as `/usr/local/bin`, is not used), without waiting for it or checking that anything appeared. With none of them (or when the one found fails), only stderr says why, so a PandaCode started from a launcher just does not open. `notify-send` shows a passing notification, not a dialog.
- On Linux the text given to `zenity`, `kdialog` or `notify-send` after a failed setup ("PandaCode could not start." and the error, which can name a file path) is a command-line argument: other local users can read it in the process list while the dialog is open, and the desktop may keep a `notify-send` notification in its history. The data folder is left out of it.
- A notice that was shown can still come back at the next launch when the app ends without `exit` and not by a crash (a termination signal or a silent kill) after the page put it on screen but before the guard read `shown`: the guard still has the `held` line and writes the notice back (`guard::put_back_held`). The window is the page's `crash_recovery_shown` call plus the line's delivery; when that send fails, `link::repair_soon` resends at once and then every 250 ms up to 20 times (about 5 s), and after that the 15 s check does, so it lasts until a resend gets through (or the guard is given up, step 3 of [How it works](#how-it-works)).
- When a quit puts an unshown notice back and the page then shows it after all, removing the put-back copy can fail (lock not had within 2 s, the save fails; logged): the next launch then shows the same notice again.
- In debug builds with `PANDACODE_CRASH_GUARD=1`, panics unwind. A caught panic is still reported on the pipe, so a silent kill within the next 10 minutes counts as a crash.

## Testing

Unit tests: `cd src-tauri && cargo test crash_guard`. `crash_guard/tests.rs` (Unix) re-runs the test binary as a fixture app with a real guard process, and checks: an aborting app is relaunched once with the restart mark, a crash is relaunched while a child still holds the pipe open, a SIGSEGV sent with `kill` ends the app and counts as a crash, a panic while another thread holds the link is reported once as a panic (one panic report, no crash report), a deliberate exit and a SIGTERM are not relaunched (and both stop agent groups still registered), a crash with restarts off leaves a notice and starts nothing, a killed guard is respawned and still relaunches, a failed relaunch is logged to the guard's private log, a relaunch is detached, and a `serve` relaunch under `xvfb-run` re-wraps and logs privately. Frontend: `node --test tests/crash-recovery.test.mjs tests/launch-restore.test.mjs` and `npx playwright test tests/crash-recovery.spec.ts`, which is in both the Chrome and the WebKit config (the preview steers `crash_*` with `window.__crashRecovery` (answered on every call and load until `crash_recovery_shown`, kept in `sessionStorage` so it outlives a reload), `__crashFailCommands`, `__crashRestartEnabled`, `__crashNoReport` (no report to reveal), `__crashGates` (a promise `crash_recovery_take` or `crash_restart_enabled` waits for), and logs calls in `__crashCalls`; it also answers `sessions_missing` with the asked ids the preview has no session for).

By hand on macOS, with a release or `--profile fast-release` build (or a debug build started with `PANDACODE_CRASH_GUARD=1`):

```sh
DATA="$HOME/Library/Application Support/ai.pandacode.desktop"
pgrep -fl pandacode            # the app and its `__crash-guard` child
kill -SEGV <app pid>           # a real SIGSEGV: relaunches after ~1.5 s with a notice and a report
ls "$DATA/crash-reports"; cat "$DATA/crash-guard.json"
kill -TERM <app pid>           # deliberate: quits, no relaunch
kill -KILL <app pid>           # silent kill: no relaunch, no report
```

`kill -SEGV` really crashes the app: the handler writes `signal 11`, puts SIGSEGV back to its default action and re-raises it, so the process dies of SIGSEGV (core dump or crash log as usual, and on macOS the "quit unexpectedly" dialog). It does not exercise the panic path; nothing in the app triggers a panic on demand. Send SIGSEGV three times within 10 minutes, or twice within 30 s of each launch, to see restarts pause. The next manual launch then shows the paused notice. Delete `crash-guard.json` to reset the history. Turn the switch off in Settings and `kill -SEGV` should write a report and leave a notice for the next manual launch, without relaunching.
