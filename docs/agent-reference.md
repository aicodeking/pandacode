## Thread window and scenes

- Thread window (`src/sky-window.ts` + `.css`, math in `src/sky-math.ts`, `tests/sky.test.mjs`, `tests/sky.spec.ts`): the main pane is a window onto a scene inside its inset: a procedural scene (WebGL fragment shader, layer `.thread-sky`, CSS gradient fallback) while the Wallpapers switch is off (the default), else the wallpaper (`.workspace-wallpaper`, now mounted in `.main-pane`). A save older than `WALLPAPER_PREFERENCES_VERSION` (3) starts off with its choices kept, so everyone sees the live scenes once; only turning the switch on saves it as on. `mountSky` sets `.workspace[data-scene="sky|wallpaper"]` (none in board/GitHub with the sky, or under Increase Contrast/Reduce Transparency/forced colours), which makes the pane clear and drives the thread's readable surfaces (`wallpapers.css` thread rules key on `data-scene`; the sky's are in `sky-window.css`). Over the sky thread text sits on the bare sky, as before the sky: no cards behind messages, traces, notes, headings, reply actions or header items; only the composer (a `--sky-glass` card, `--bg` at 92% with a blur) and the user's bubble keep a fill. Readability comes from the grade and the ink: light keeps every pixel at Y ≥ `lightFloor(night)` (.46 by day, .44 at night: a pale moonlit periwinkle), dark at Y ≤ `DARK_CEILING` (.06, the sun, moon and stars held to it too, a calm dim disc), the ink steps further (light `--muted`/`--faint` #3a3a36, and a message's meta and the preview note 60% further toward `--text` so they clear ~5.5:1 at night; dark #c2c2c2/#c0c0c0, status tones toward white), and a soft page-tone halo (`text-shadow`/`drop-shadow`) steadies glyphs over cloud texture; `tests/sky.spec.ts` checks that those items draw no fill and measures text (4.5:1) and icons (3:1) against the worst sampled pixel behind them at four times in both themes. Sky: colours and sun/moon arcs from the local clock (`skyColorsAt`, `sunPosition`, `moonPosition`; `?sky=HH:MM` pins it in the preview; bodies ride y .3–.44 (moon to .38), the open middle above the composer), a cirrus layer plus three parallax cumulus layers (domain-warped fbm eroded at the edges, cover varying across the sky, a 4-step linear Beer-Lambert march toward the sun or moon, its start jittered per pixel (interleaved gradient noise) so low light never bands, plus one sunward detail sample, silver lining near the light, clearing and forward scatter near the sun so no cloud bites the disc, whose core is never darker than its bloom; lit/shade pairs keep a wide warm/cool gap at dawn and dusk), a sun disc with bloom (in dark a calm disc: core Y ≤ .6, a softer bloom), stars and a crescent at night (its shadowed limb feathers into the sky, the tight halo faded off it); rendered at 0.6 device px (.45 under the governor; ≥0.75 per CSS px). The theme grade is hue-preserving: light lifts Y to ≥ `lightFloor(night)`, dark compresses to ≤ `DARK_CEILING` and pulls daytime toward saturated navy with slate clouds (warm daytime tops silvered, lit/shade spread .5–1.5×), a dusk horizon fades toward grey near the bottom and the rose turn is .3, gated by Y (sun, moon, stars excepted), the grey fade weak by day; cover thins a little at night; a 1/255 dither keeps halos from banding. Wind follows `#transcript[data-running]` on a ~1 s spring (`data-wind`, in tenths rounded up, so it is written ~10 times per change, not per frame) that gets a small push on the flip, so it moves on the next frame; the pointer parts the clouds and brightens them only where the sky shows (passive `pointermove`; over `READABLE` surfaces — messages, traces, the composer, header, dock — it lets go; position on an underdamped spring, ζ .7, settling ~0.5 s after it rests), ~8 fps at rest on a `setTimeout` (never rAF), 30 on a timer while only the wind moves and 60 on rAF while the pointer is live and the parting closes fast, then 30 for its slow end (`data-sky-state="moving"` from the wake on); size comes from the ResizeObserver (paint reads no layout); a governor samples paint time (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query` where present, else the rAF interval past a 60 Hz slot) and drops the render scale .6 → .45 when 30 samples average over 8 ms, back only after 60 s under 4 ms (`__sky.scale`, `__sky.timing` in the preview, and `__sky.gpuMs`, the mean of the last 10 GPU timer results, null without the extension; `__sky.forcePaintMs`/`__sky.quietMs` drive it in the spec); data attributes and gradient vars are written only on change, paused (`data-sky-state`) when hidden, blurred, covered (Settings, board, GitHub, an inert pane) or off screen, one still frame every 3 min under reduced motion; DPR capped at 2, rendered at half scale. `window.__sky.frames` (preview only) counts paints. Scenes (`src/scenes/`): every `src/scenes/*.ts` exporting `scene: Scene` registers itself (`index.ts`, `import.meta.glob`, sorted by id; no shared edit to add one). `types.ts` holds the contract in its top comment: the host compiles `PRELUDE` (uniforms `res aspect drift time wind ptr ptrVel ptrAmt dark floorY ceilY night sun moon top mid hor glow lit shade`, plus `noise`/`fbm`/`lin`/`gradeLight`/`gradeDark`/`finish`) + the scene's `fragment` lazily, one program per scene in one context; a scene keeps light Y ≥ `floorY` and dark Y ≤ `ceilY` (hue-preserving), ends with `finish()` (1/255 dither), moves with `drift`/`wind`, answers the pointer only where it shows, stays within ~1.3× the sky's cost, and gives a `fallback` gradient. Eight scenes: `sky` (described above), `alpine` (ranges and pines, rose and peach at dawn and dusk), `aurora` (polar night: curtains over snowy ranges, stars; in light, tints reaching mid-sky that follow the clock), `dunes` (knife-edged ranges, airborne sand on `drift` at rest, sand streamers and walking ripples on the wind), `lagoon` (underwater: surface band, god rays, a caustic floor, bubbles), `nebula` (gas, dust lanes, a lens at the pointer), `ocean` (swells and a glitter path; a high veil on the wind; rings at the pointer, which thins the veil and reflects when over the sky), `rain` (fogged glass: bokeh gathered in city blocks, sizes varying by depth, sliding drops, and the pointer wipes a clear streak). After the first paint `precompile()` compiles the unused scenes one per idle slot (`requestIdleCallback`, never within 1 s of input or while the pointer stirs; with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` the driver compiles in the background and a later slot collects it, polled every 100 ms), so a first visit draws without a compile stall (`__sky.compiled` lists them). Each thread shows `sceneFor(id)` (FNV-1a + avalanche of the session id mod the count, `scenes/pick.ts`, `tests/scenes.test.mjs`); no thread (new-task page, drafts, shared channels) shows the sky. `renderTranscript` calls `setThread` via `showThreadScene` in `main.ts`; a change crossfades: the old scene's last frame is copied to `.thread-sky-fade` (2D canvas) and faded out over 300 ms with WAAPI (opacity only, `data-fading` while it runs, `__sky.fades` counts them), instant on the first thread, before `data-ready`, under reduced motion, while paused or without WebGL. The layer carries `data-thread-scene`; `data-scene="sky"` on the workspace still means any procedural scene. `?scene=<id>` pins one scene everywhere (preview); `tests/sky.spec.ts` runs its readability checks for every registered scene, `tests/scenes.spec.ts` covers the pick, crossfade, pin and reduced motion.

## Motion

- Motion: subtle and never in the way. `MOTION = true` in `src/motion.ts` drives surfaces (dialogs, toast, panes; menus arrive via `popover-motion.ts` and leave at once) with short critically damped springs: a few pixels of travel, ~120–220 ms of visible movement, exits ~120 ms; input is never gated and every caller keeps an instant path (`MOTION = false` makes the app instant again). CSS transitions are limited to color, background, opacity, box-shadow and `scale`/`rotate` in 100–200 ms (`translate` only for switch knobs: a few composited pixels), timed with `--ds-ease-in` (120 ms, hover in) / `--ds-ease-out` (180 ms) and `ease-out` (defined in `styles.css`); never animate layout (width, height, grid, top/left). A theme switch closes the same gate for two frames (`setTheme` in `theme.ts`), so the palette lands at once. Segment thumbs' size FLIP plays only when the selection changed (`data-segment-last`), never on re-entry or resize. Neither empty state (`Choose a project`, `Open a project to get started`) draws until the first `list_projects` has settled (`projectsListed` in `main.ts`). Nothing transitions on first paint: `design-system.css` turns transitions off under `html:not([data-ready])` (and the first-paint keyframes of `.turn-change-tree` and `.history-loading`, which stays hidden until the gate opens), and `palette.ts` sets `data-ready` two frames after its last startup palette write, and the first wallpaper scene (decoded after that) closes the gate again and reopens it two frames later (`holdStartupGate` in `wallpapers.ts`). Switches (`.ds-toggle`, `.settings-switch`, `role=switch` checkboxes) slide only once armed (`data-armed`, set by one delegated pointerdown/keydown listener in `palette.ts`), so a state set during render never animates; `morph()` keeps `data-armed`, and the token saver panel and a bot's settings pane redraw with `morph()` so their switches survive to slide (`resetFields` in `bots.ts` then matches each kept field to its markup). Menus (`showPopover` in `src/popover-motion.ts`) arrive with opacity and `scale` only, and glass that materializes (`materializeIn`/`materializeOut` in motion.ts: toast, agents flyout) moves only opacity and transform; never an animated backdrop-filter. Segment thumbs slide on `--spring-fast`; disclosure chevrons (sidebar sections, folders, Bots, Changes, file tree) turn with the `rotate` property on `--spring-fast`, and their redraws go through `morph()` (which keeps the shared start of a list that grew or shrank at its end) so the chevron survives to turn; a section-fold heading swaps its count and chevron by opacity over `--ds-ease-in`. A steer bar that appears during a run, and the approval bar going from empty to filled in the open thread (`approvalBarShown`, since the composer rebuilds its host; never on a thread switch), fade up with `settleUnfolded`. A `.support-spinner` fades in only after 150 ms (`support-appear`), so short work never flashes one. A pulse is `state-pulse` (`styles.css`, opacity only, 1.6s, infinite, inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; every state pulse uses it, thinking dots, the saver switch's busy knob and the bots skeleton included and `syncPulses()` in motion.ts starts each on the timeline's zero, so all breathe in phase, and the launcher has its own copy, since its window does not load that sheet; its worker and subagent rows run in `--launcher-team`, from `--ds-hue-team`). State colours ease with `transition: color|background-color var(--ds-ease-out)`, so a kept node fades and replaced markup lands at once; no keyframes on arrival. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes CSS transitions and animations (`styles.css`, `overlay.css`) and motion.ts keeps only a short cross-fade for surfaces (dialogs, toast, panes); `enterView` and `settleIn` play nothing, so a send or a thread switch runs no animation. The live tool trace (`src/trace-motion.ts`, `src/trace-motion.css`) choreographs more: steps blur in, the two newest result cards unfold (older ones fold), the header mirrors the running step with a shimmer, and the turn folds to its summary at the end, with @keyframes and the Web Animations API; the action dock (`src/action-dock.ts`) follows the same rules. Press feedback uses the `scale` property, never `transform`, so a button positioned with `transform` stays under the pointer while pressed. Sidebar task rows are `.task-row` wrappers holding the `.task-item` and its `.task-actions` (the `.task-more` row menu); the selected row has a slim accent bar inside its leading edge (`left: 0`, opacity 180 ms and `scale` 180 ms on `--spring-fast`); row colours ease in over 120 ms and out over 180 ms, each property with its own duration so the press `scale` keeps `--spring-fast-duration`. Shell motion (`src/motion.ts`): a thread switch plays `settleIn()` once, after the real content draws (a remembered page at once, otherwise when the page arrives, never on the loading placeholder): opacity from 0.7 following the spring (~200 ms) with a 4px rise, continuing from what is on screen and at its current speed if one is already running, so rapid switching never dims the transcript again or rises in steps; the GitHub view fades in the same way (opacity only) once `mountGithubHub` has drawn, as do the Settings content once it has drawn and on each section change, the transcript when Settings closes, and the conversation pane on returning to the Conversation view; a sidebar collapse/expand settles `.conversation-pane` from 0.85 (opacity only; the width lands at once; never `.main-pane`, whose opacity would re-composite the header's and dock's backdrop blur). On a first visit (no remembered page) the previous thread's rows stay for at most 150 ms (`holdTranscript` in `main.ts`, `holdDim`/`releaseHold` in motion.ts): `#transcript` turns `inert` and dims to 0.7 (~120 ms, from what is on screen); the page arriving releases the hold into `settleIn` from the live opacity (above 0.9 the hold is cancelled and the page fades from 0.85, so a fast first visit is never a hard cut), and any other exit (error, welcome, shared channel) ends it; after 150 ms the loading line (`.history-loading`) takes over at once (`.after-hold`). ## Providers and harnesses

- Turn speed and tokens: the fixed app instructions (sidebar titles, `pandacode-run.sh`, change tracking; `app_instructions()` in `lib.rs`) go to Claude-family harnesses once as `--append-system-prompt`, which caches with the prefix; other harnesses get them in the message on a new conversation and every 8th turn. Only a one-line current-title note rides each message. After a successful Claude turn, `providers/warm.rs` starts the next turn's process in the background (at most 2, 10 idle minutes); a follow-up adopts it only when model, mode, effort, folder, resume ID, instructions, tool catalog and saved executable all match (the MCP server is rebound to the new turn at the same address), so it skips the CLI's startup. Anything else starts cold. A run stays open while its background Agent subagents run, so they report back before `done` (`providers/steering.rs`); a shell a subagent left running holds it at most 5 minutes (`CHILD_SHELL_HOLD`), then the main agent gets one notice turn and the run ends with a note. An empty `result` for a leftover background task never ends a run before its prompt is taken. The team scheduler is event-driven plus a 30 s `architect/watchdog.rs` tick that reaps orphaned workers and survives one team's errors.
- Harnesses: adding a provider means touching every `"kimi" | "opencode" | "antigravity"`-style match in `providers.rs`/`acp.rs`, the allowlists in `storage.rs`, `overlay.rs`, `architect*.rs`, `terminal.rs`, `slash_commands*.rs`, `provider_settings.rs`, plus `ProviderId` and its `Record` maps on the frontend (tsc flags the maps). ACP harnesses share `acp.rs`; provider quirks (Grok's `authenticate` step, plan-mode policy, `_meta.totalTokens` usage) live behind `config.provider` checks there. Record real frames in the tests, and keep an ignored live smoke test per installed CLI. ## Remote access and headless

- Remote access, PandaCode Connect ([docs/remote-access.md](docs/remote-access.md)), is opt-in: there is no network traffic until GitHub sign-in (device flow). The relay is `relay/`, a Cloudflare Worker with one SQLite Durable Object per account, hibernated WebSockets, and GitHub app-checked sign-in. It forwards only X25519 + XChaCha20-Poly1305 ciphertext. The native side is `src-tauri/src/remote*`: a host answers only devices in its account's presence, and only while "Allow remote control" is on (no per-device pinning; Full access for remote tasks is a separate, default-off host setting). The relay token and device key live in `connect-secrets.json` (0600, `remote/secrets.rs`), never the keychain: macOS re-prompts for keychain items after every unsigned update. `secret_store.rs` shares its file code (`load`/`update`: 0600 temp, fsync, rename, lock file). Requests, responses and events carry `ts`/`nonce` against replay. A task can *run on another computer* as a mirror: an ordinary local session row with `remote_device`/`remote_session`/`remote_synced_id` (schema 26), started by `remote_start_task` (host side `remote/start.rs` finds or clones the GitHub project into `~/PandaCode`). Agent commands for a mirror (`send_message`, `stop_session`, approvals, input, `steer_task`, model/effort) are routed natively by `remote::route`; the queue stays local. Hosts push raw `AgentEvent`s (`agent.events`, one frame per subscriber per 100 ms, text deltas merged, then `run.finished`), which the viewer re-emits as `agent-event` under the local id, and messages are copied with `task.messages` after each run (`remote/mirror.rs`). `Store::workspace_root` refuses a mirror (`This task runs on <name>.`), so git, worktree and file commands never touch the local disk. There is no remote view. Machine orchestration ([docs/machine-orchestration.md](docs/machine-orchestration.md), off by default) lets agents link other computers (`Settings.links`) and launch mirrors as child threads (`machine_threads`) through `pandacode_machine_*` tools in `remote/orchestrate.rs` (composed by `with_machines` beside `with_vm`, never for team tasks; `machine_launch` and `machine_message` ask for approval unless the task runs in Full access, like every PandaCode host tool; the link's access still caps the child thread's mode, and a Read-only link refuses them; `machine_stop` never asks); `mirror::commit` is the one start path for both `remote_start_task` and `machine_launch` and re-checks the link and parent mode at the commit point (`commit_mode`), and settled threads wake their idle parent once with a `MachineThreads` app-origin turn (`launch_app_turn` in `lib.rs`), claimed under the run's admission lock and marked reported in the turn's own transaction (`Store::begin_task_turn_reporting`); due wakes retry at run end, reopen, startup and re-enable (`orchestrate::reconcile`). Every machine tool answers within 45 s (harness tool calls time out at 60 s): link requests live 10 min in `links.rs` apart from the call (an answer nobody waited for wakes the task with a `MachineLink` turn), and `machine_launch` commits the row and spawns `mirror::start` in the same step (`mirror::commit`); a machine thread's `startId` is its own id, so its lost reply follows main's `task.startStatus` path and a start a quit cut off is followed up again after restart (`mirror::load_starts`; `Store::open` keeps such rows instead of turning them back into drafts); a stop while starting ends the thread here at once and sends `task.cancelStart`, and a failed start keeps the thread as `error` (`fail_start`, from `mirror::not_started`). A link counts only while the device hosts or is offline and still has the key it was linked under (`Link::key`); a stored link whose device left the account still serves `read_access`, so `status_of` lists it (`departed`, named from `Link::name`) and Settings offers only Unlink. `machine_launch` takes `machine: "auto"` (`pick_machine`); `catalog::warm` fills linked computers' catalogs on link and presence, off while orchestration is off. A running thread on a host offline past `links::HOST_OFFLINE_AFTER` (5 min) reports `hostOffline` and wakes an idle parent once all its running threads are on such hosts: that turn sets `reported_by` but leaves `reported` 0, so the thread reports normally if it ends later. `claim` reads presence from a snapshot, never the remote lock (it holds the store lock). `remote/loopback.rs` runs the viewer's start path against the host's start logic on two in-memory stores; `remote::bench::live_two_computers` is the documented real run. Wakes are at-least-once: a wake turn that never reached its agent (no assistant or tool message after it) gives its claim back (`orchestrate::restore_wake`, at run end, on a failed prepare and at startup in `recover_wakes`). A parent interrupted by a restart can be woken; one the user stopped cannot, even after a quit right after Stop (`stop_requested`). Machine approvals wait at most `orchestrate::APPROVAL_WAIT` (240 s) on both the MCP host and Codex dynamic tools (`expire_approvals`), and the MCP host ends a call whose client closed the connection, so a late Approve runs nothing. `remote.json` saves take turns under `Remote::saving` and write the current state.
 The UI is `src/remote*.ts`, with mocks in `src/preview-remote.ts`. Builds need `PANDACODE_RELAY_URL` and `PANDACODE_GITHUB_CLIENT_ID`.
- Headless host ([docs/headless.md](docs/headless.md)): `pandacode serve` (dispatched in `main.rs`, `handle_serve`) runs the same app on an always-on server with no window and no frontend — `setup_common` in `lib.rs` is the desktop startup minus GUI (overlay/tray/appearance/VM watch), `on_run_event` is the shared exit handler (a window close never stops serve; SIGTERM/SIGINT do, with the same 12 s agent deadline), and both build paths share one `app_context()` because a second `generate_context!` re-embeds the Info.plist static. `src-tauri/src/headless.rs` holds the CLI (`--sign-in` device flow on stdout via `remote.rs`'s headless helpers, `--sign-out`, `--full-access`, `--device-name`) and the native twins of the window's real jobs: queue drain (`drain_queue`, mirroring the bot gateway and frontend `dispatchQueue`), pull-request links and limit resumes (`Store::due_limit_resumes`) at run end / on a timer, and a fail-fast for app-action tools. Frontend behaviors with real effects need a native twin there — see the docs checklist. `drain_queue` is sync-on-purpose (spawns internally): awaiting the send chain through a second async fn trips a rustc Send-resolution ambiguity. `PANDACODE_SECRETS_FILE=1` (set by serve by default) selects the macOS-style 0600 file backend on Linux/Windows too (`secret_store::file_mode`). Bots are configured headlessly with `serve --import-bots <file>` (or dumped with `--export-bots`): `headless_bots.rs` deserializes each entry straight into the UI's `BotInput`/`ChannelInput`/`ScheduleInput` and applies through the same constructors (`bots::create/update/set_capabilities`, `channels::add`, `schedule::create`, `guard::add_rule`), matching by name/kind+label/title/pattern so re-applying converges; the wiring then starts added channels live via `channels::start` (channel loops snapshot config at boot, so hand-written rows need a restart).

## Bots and chats

- Bots (docs/bots.md) live in `src-tauri/src/bots*` and `src/bots*.ts`. Each bot owns a workspace project (kept out of the project tree, filter and picker by `userProjects()`); each conversation is an ordinary task with the normal transcript, streaming, queue, composer and model picker, and `run_finished`/`approval_requested` hooks in `lib.rs` route replies and approvals back to channels. Prompts typed in a bot's own conversation go through `bot_message` so the brief, memories and soul apply; the brief folds behind the message's attached context. The Bots sidebar groups Needs you, Pinned, Bots and Hidden; statuses cover approvals/questions, usage limits, work, unread replies and completed runs. Its plus menu offers New bot and New channel. Profile settings (saved as you go) hold identity, an optional soul (personality Markdown, `bots.soul`), harness/model under Brain and permission modes under Rules; a Skills section picks library skills and MCP servers by reference (`bots.skills`/`bots.mcp`, `bot_set_capabilities`), and a project task can start with a bot (`bot_tasks`, `botTaskBrief` in `main.ts`); per-bot external Channels are labeled Connections. Failover can hold a prompt for another model, with decisions above the composer and model-switch transcript markers. Shared bot channels (like `#launch`) have ordered members and a lead; posts route to the lead or up to two @mentioned members with the channel's recent history, replies return as authored rows, assignments and bot messages start queued and turn in progress only when their turn launches (`promote_taken`), and users can stop (including queued assignments), continue or reassign stopped or failed posts; failover keeps a delivery open until the backup run ends (a dismissed hold nothing resumes settles it as failed; Stop drops a held delivery's card), each run end settles the exact request its launch promoted (`shared_channels::Running`; a failed launch puts it back to queued), a bot reset/delete settles its posts as stopped, and member removal or archiving stops pending work. Channel history is paged (`HISTORY_PAGE`). The channel header edits name/members/lead; archived channels are preview-only (no posts or settings until restored). Bot tasks can use `bot_message_bot(to, text)` to message another bot's desktop task; both threads show expandable, status-labeled markers and the reply returns as `Update from <bot>`. Self-messages are rejected and chains stop at three hops. Shared-channel and message tables live in `bots::SCHEMA` and idempotent `bots::upgrade()`; no app storage schema bump. Connections' secrets stay in `secret_store` via `bots/secrets.rs` and never reach the webview; the local web chat server binds to 127.0.0.1 and checks Host.
- Projectless chats ([docs/chats.md](docs/chats.md)): `chat_create` (`src-tauri/src/chats.rs`) makes `~/PandaCode Chats/chat-<id>/`, registers it as a project named Chat and creates the task; a project is a chat by folder location (`isChatProject` in `src/chats.ts`), so `userProjects()` hides it and the sidebar lists its tasks under Chats. Chat tasks hide the project bar, Deploy and Run (`body.chat-thread`), and `app_instructions(true)` drops the run-script text. After deleting or moving a chat task, `chat_cleanup` forgets the project and removes the folder only if nothing but PandaCode's files remain.

- Workflow recipes ([docs/recipes.md](docs/recipes.md)): JSON step lists in `.pandacode/recipes/` or app data (`recipes.rs`), opened from the composer `+` menu (`openRecipes()` in `main.ts`, `src/recipes.ts`). A dry run marks each step supported, fallback, manual or blocked for the task's harness; running queues the steps through the ordinary message queue.
- Turn checks (`src/turn-checks.ts`, `checks.rs`, off by default in Settings → Agents): after a clean turn the app runs the checkout's own fast test/check command with the user's environment, bounded by `run_process`; a failure queues one editable fix message, never a loop, and bot tasks are skipped.

## Preview mocks

- Browser preview mocks for new IPC commands live in `src/preview.ts` (`previewWorktree`, `previewGithub`); add one for every new command so the Playwright suites can cover it.

## Mobile app

- Mobile app (`mobile/`, Expo, iOS + Android): a viewer and controller for PandaCode Connect that never runs tasks. `src/connect/` is the client: crypto byte-compatible with `remote/crypto.rs`, the relay socket, trust pins and RPC. `src/store/` is zustand plus a pure `reduce.ts` for live events; it ignores the host's raw `done` and ends a run on `run.finished`. Screens are in `src/app/`: Inbox, Computers and Settings are tabs of the system tab bar (`(tabs)/_layout.tsx`, expo-router `NativeTabs`: a real UITabBarController with Liquid Glass and minimize on scroll, Material bottom navigation on Android), each its own Stack; the Inbox is one inbox across all computers (`(tabs)/inbox/index.tsx`, sections in `src/store/inbox.ts`: Running first, then Needs you with approvals and questions before failures and limits, then Recent; running rows show `Working 6m` or `Stalled 4m` after `STALL_MS` without an event (`live.lastEventAt`, else the host's `lastEventAt`), the current tool or Thinking from `glanceOf`, and `+N agents`; only the small `RunClock` ticks, every 15 s, and `client.ts` also subscribes to every listed running task (`glance`, at most 20) so the inbox sees their events). Traces match the desktop: `src/store/trace.ts` ports `src/tool-trace.ts` (kinds, sentences, edit diffs, todos, subagent snapshots, tool groups) and `src/ui/Trace.tsx` draws tool rows (icon, status, duration, tap for diff/checklist/command output/raw input and output), subagent panels, the run's Plan card and the Thinking line; `reduce.ts` keeps tool start/end times, `subagents`, `todos`, `thinking`, `startedAt`, a limit past its run end, and merges newer hosts' `lastEventAt`/`runStartedAt`/`subagents` (`withHostActivity`; older hosts send none). The task screen and the New task, Task info and Model & permissions sheets stay in the root Stack and cover the tab bar. The task screen is at `task/[device]/[session].tsx` with a native Stack header (`src/ui/TaskHeader.tsx`: title, system back, and `Stack.Toolbar` SF Symbol items for Model & permissions, Task info and Stop while running; transparent so the wallpaper shows behind the title; the inverted transcript scrolls under the bar (top padding = header height) with iOS's automatic scroll edge effect turned off (`scrollEdgeEffects` hidden on every edge: sized from the flipped list it blurred most of the transcript), and `HeaderBlur` draws a systemChromeMaterial blur with a soft fade and hairline behind the bar on Liquid Glass systems; a non-translucent bar always paints opaque whatever `headerStyle` says). Sent prompts show at once as optimistic bubbles (`src/store/sent.ts`: Sending…, Steer · Sending…, Queued) replaced by the first matching saved user message; the phone also fetches messages when a watched task turns running and after a steer. Threads open newest first (`loadMessages` in `client.ts`): the store holds each thread's newest window, drawn at once from the disk cache (`src/store/threads.ts`, pure `thread-cache.ts`: one hashed file per device+session in cache/threads/ with the last 200 messages, written 1.5 s after a change, 8 MB total with LRU eviction, cleared on sign-out), else a `task.messages {tail: true, limit: 60}` page behind a grey skeleton; afterId pages then catch up ("Couldn't refresh" with Retry if that fails) and `{beforeId}` pages load as the inverted list nears the top (`loadOlder`, `older` in the store). Hosts whose replies lack `hasOlder` (or refuse the params) are remembered in `legacy` and paged by afterId as before (`src/connect/paging.ts`). Every tasks.list queues a background prefetch (`src/store/prefetch.ts`: running, waiting, then each host's 5 newest, skipping threads whose cached id matches the row's `lastId`), one call at a time and held while a thread is open or a prompt sends; inbox rows start the cache read on press-in. `src/connect/perf.ts` logs dev-only `[thread-perf]` tap→paint→current timings. Live Activities (`src/live/`, `expo-widgets`: Lock Screen card and Dynamic Island, extension `com.aicodeking.pandacode.LiveActivity`, App Group `group.com.aicodeking.pandacode`) summarise running/waiting tasks while the app is alive; background updates need APNs (`docs/app-store/live-activities.md`), turns folded by `src/store/turns.ts` and a glass composer. Liquid Glass comes from `expo-glass-effect`, with solid fallbacks. Sends made while offline wait in the outbox (`src/store/outbox.ts`, kept in SecureStore) and go out in order on reconnect. Snoozes and pins are phone-only. Harness logos are copied from `src/assets/providers/`. On the iPhone Duo iOS itself moves the status bar, the tab bar and native header items (never custom header views) into a vertical rail on one edge; there is no custom rail, and nothing may pad the root layout or the tab controller won't reach the edge. Screens keep content clear of side insets themselves (`contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="automatic"` plus `insets.left/right` on lists, the composer dock and floating views; never a fixed tab bar height). When the rail is drawn on a wide window (`useSplit()` in `src/ui/split.tsx`: `useVerticalBarEdgeIOS() != null` and width >= 700) the app becomes a split view: the inbox sidebar (`Sidebar.tsx`, rendered by the root layout on every route so the stack never resizes during a tab switch, which drops the tabs from the rail) beside the selected task or a New thread pane (`NewThread.tsx`); the sidebar toggle and New task are native header items. Every other phone keeps the single-column layout. The desktop's 21 wallpaper scenes are copied to `mobile/assets/wallpapers/` and drawn once behind every screen (`src/ui/Backdrop.tsx`; `src/ui/wallpaper.ts` is the same schedule as `src/wallpapers.ts`, and `tests/wallpaper.test.ts` compares them), with visibility, blur and scene in Settings (`WallpaperSettings.tsx`); the phone starts at 26 % and 2 px (`phoneWallpaperPreferences` in `wallpaper.ts`, which leaves the shared schedule alone and moves old saves still at the untouched 20/0; saves carry `version: 2`). Screens and the tab controller are transparent so the glass refracts it; Reduce Transparency turns it off. Settings → Appearance (System/Light/Dark, `src/ui/appearance.ts`, in SecureStore) calls `Appearance.setColorScheme`, so native headers, tabs, sheets, glass, blur, keyboard and status bar follow; colours come from `theme.ts` tokens/PlatformColor, and black harness tiles (`black` in `harness.tsx`) sit on a light rim in dark mode. Project tiles (`ProjectTile.tsx`: inbox rows, sidebar, New thread/New task pickers, Task info) show the host's `projects.icons` data: URLs via `connect.projectIcons` (batches of 50, after `tasks.list` for listed tasks' projects and for the picked project; cached per device+project in memory and `project-icons.json` in the cache directory, refetched daily, missing ids retried after 10 min, hosts answering `Unknown method.` skipped for the session; pure logic in `src/store/icon-cache.ts`), else the coloured initial. `EXPO_PUBLIC_PANDACODE_DEMO=1` seeds fake data for screenshots. The task header also carries a pull request item (`arrow.triangle.pull`, shown once `connect.loadPull` finds a linked PR via `task.pull`, else `task.pullDetect`; cached in `pulls`, rechecked at run end), Changes (`plus.forwardslash.minus`) and a More menu (Model & permissions, Task info), all `Stack.Toolbar` items; they push `pull.tsx` (state, checks, labels, body, files, commits, timeline, Merge with a confirmed merge/squash/rebase, Open on GitHub), `changes.tsx` (`task.changes` plus counts from one `task.diff`, pull to refresh, reloaded at run end) and `diff.tsx` (`task.pullDiff`/`task.diff` for one path in `src/ui/DiffView.tsx`; line kinds in `src/store/diff.ts`). Bots: `bots.list` runs after each `tasks.list`; rows with a `botId` leave the project sections and the inbox and Duo sidebar list each bot under Bots (`botRows` in `src/store/inbox.ts`, `src/ui/BotRow.tsx`); a bot opens its desktop conversation (`bot.thread`) in the ordinary task screen, whose composer sends through `bot.message` and whose header shows the avatar, name and Pause/Resume (`bot.pause`). Older hosts answer `Unknown method.`: `newer()` in `client.ts` marks the host `outdated` and the screen says "Update PandaCode on <computer> to use this." The demo answers all of these from `src/demo-host.ts` and `src/demo-pull.ts`. PandaCode's own MCP tools (team coordination, title, track_files, simulator, VM, design, preview) read as sentences from `src/store/pandacode-tools.ts` (`Dispatched 3 workers`, `Asked GPT-6 for a follow-up`, `Tapped (200, 618)`; worker names from the task's team through `WorkerNamesContext`), with icons in `Trace.tsx` and cards in `src/ui/ToolCards.tsx` (an assignment card per dispatched worker, the finish summary and team messages as Markdown, a checkpoint, a screenshot when the result carries its bytes); other MCP calls read `server › tool` with pretty-printed arguments. Agents: the task header's `person.3` item (shown when the task has subagents or team workers) and the inbox's `+N agents` badge push `agents.tsx` (Team workers and Subagents: harness logo, model, status, elapsed time, current tool from the worker's own watched session, one-line summary), and a row pushes `agent.tsx` (a worker's checkpoint, summary, files and `task.workerMessages` transcript, refreshed every 5 s while it runs; a subagent's prompt, tools and reply). Teams come from `task.team` (`src/store/team.ts` decodes it and applies the `team` live event's changed worker row; `{ removed: true }` drops it) into `teams` in the store, asked once when a task opens and every 5 s on the Agents screens while the team works (`src/ui/useTeam.ts`); a host without `task.team` is remembered for the session apart from `outdated` and the screen says to update it. The demo's architect task (`src/demo-team.ts`) has three workers (done, running with a checkpoint, failed) and dispatch/finish/message calls. It relies on the host methods `tasks.list`, `task.start` with `projectId`, and `pending` in `subscribe`. Attachments: newer hosts send `attachments` rows on task.messages (`parseAttachments`/`bubbleAttachments` in `src/store/attachments.ts`; rows or images without an id, and older hosts, keep the name chips parsed from the prompt); user bubbles show images as a thumbnail grid (`src/ui/Attachments.tsx`) that opens `src/ui/ImageViewer.tsx` (native modal, pinch/double-tap zoom, swipe between images, swipe down to close, Share on iOS), and document/folder chips open the message's own context block in a monospace sheet. Bytes come through `task.attachment`/`task.workspaceImage` chunks (`src/connect/attachments.ts`: one download at a time for the app, each chunk after the thread's own message calls, prefetch held while any waits, concurrent asks deduped, 'Unknown method.' remembered per host) into cache/attachments/ (`src/store/attachment-files.ts`: `.part` files resume, 64 MB LRU, cleared on sign-out). Agent replies never hand images to the native Markdown renderer (it would fetch remote ones): `replyParts` draws workspace-relative `![alt](path)` as tappable thumbnails and turns anything with a scheme into a link. The demo's docs thread carries two bundled images and a document (`src/demo-images.ts`). Verify with `cd mobile && npx tsc --noEmit && npm test && npx expo lint`. `/ios` and `/android` are generated by prebuild and are not committed.
