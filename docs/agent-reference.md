## Thread window and scenes

- Thread window (`src/sky-window.ts` + `.css`, math in `src/sky-math.ts`, `tests/sky.test.mjs`, `tests/sky.spec.ts`): the main pane is a window onto a scene inside its inset: a procedural scene (WebGL fragment shader, layer `.thread-sky`, CSS gradient fallback) while the Wallpapers switch is off (the default), else the wallpaper (`.workspace-wallpaper`, now mounted in `.main-pane`). A save older than `WALLPAPER_PREFERENCES_VERSION` (3) starts off with its choices kept, so everyone sees the live scenes once; only turning the switch on saves it as on. `mountSky` sets `.workspace[data-scene="sky|wallpaper"]` (none in board/GitHub with the sky, or under Increase Contrast/Reduce Transparency/forced colours), which makes the pane clear and drives the thread's readable surfaces (`wallpapers.css` thread rules key on `data-scene`; the sky's are in `sky-window.css`). Over the sky thread text sits on the bare sky, as before the sky: no cards behind messages, traces, notes, headings, reply actions or header items; only the composer (a `--sky-glass` card, `--bg` at 92% with a blur) and the user's bubble keep a fill. Readability comes from the grade and the ink: light keeps every pixel at Y ≥ `lightFloor(night)` (.46 by day, .44 at night: a pale moonlit periwinkle), dark at Y ≤ `DARK_CEILING` (.06, the sun, moon and stars held to it too, a calm dim disc), the ink steps further (light `--muted`/`--faint` #3a3a36, and a message's meta and the preview note 60% further toward `--text` so they clear ~5.5:1 at night; dark #c2c2c2/#c0c0c0, status tones toward white), and a soft page-tone halo (`text-shadow`/`drop-shadow`) steadies glyphs over cloud texture; `tests/sky.spec.ts` checks that those items draw no fill and measures text (4.5:1) and icons (3:1) against the worst sampled pixel behind them at four times in both themes. Sky: colours and sun/moon arcs from the local clock (`skyColorsAt`, `sunPosition`, `moonPosition`; `?sky=HH:MM` pins it in the preview; bodies ride y .3–.44 (moon to .38), the open middle above the composer), a cirrus layer plus three parallax cumulus layers (domain-warped fbm eroded at the edges, cover varying across the sky, a 4-step linear Beer-Lambert march toward the sun or moon, its start jittered per pixel (interleaved gradient noise) so low light never bands, plus one sunward detail sample, silver lining near the light, clearing and forward scatter near the sun so no cloud bites the disc, whose core is never darker than its bloom; lit/shade pairs keep a wide warm/cool gap at dawn and dusk), a sun disc with bloom (in dark a calm disc: core Y ≤ .6, a softer bloom), stars and a crescent at night (its shadowed limb feathers into the sky, the tight halo faded off it); rendered at 0.6 device px (.45 under the governor; ≥0.75 per CSS px). The theme grade is hue-preserving: light lifts Y to ≥ `lightFloor(night)`, dark compresses to ≤ `DARK_CEILING` and pulls daytime toward saturated navy with slate clouds (warm daytime tops silvered, lit/shade spread .5–1.5×), a dusk horizon fades toward grey near the bottom and the rose turn is .3, gated by Y (sun, moon, stars excepted), the grey fade weak by day; cover thins a little at night; a 1/255 dither keeps halos from banding. Wind follows `#transcript[data-running]` on a ~1 s spring (`data-wind`, in tenths rounded up, so it is written ~10 times per change, not per frame) that gets a small push on the flip, so it moves on the next frame; the pointer parts the clouds and brightens them only where the sky shows (passive `pointermove`; over `READABLE` surfaces — messages, traces, the composer, header, dock — it lets go; position on an underdamped spring, ζ .7, settling ~0.5 s after it rests), ~8 fps at rest on a `setTimeout` (never rAF), 30 on a timer while only the wind moves and 60 on rAF while the pointer is live and the parting closes fast, then 30 for its slow end (`data-sky-state="moving"` from the wake on); size comes from the ResizeObserver (paint reads no layout); a governor samples paint time (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query` where present, else the rAF interval past a 60 Hz slot) and drops the render scale .6 → .45 when 30 samples average over 8 ms, back only after 60 s under 4 ms (`__sky.scale`, `__sky.timing` in the preview, and `__sky.gpuMs`, the mean of the last 10 GPU timer results, null without the extension; `__sky.forcePaintMs`/`__sky.quietMs` drive it in the spec); data attributes and gradient vars are written only on change, paused (`data-sky-state`) when hidden, blurred, covered (Settings, board, GitHub, an inert pane) or off screen, one still frame every 3 min under reduced motion; DPR capped at 2, rendered at half scale. `window.__sky.frames` (preview only) counts paints. Scenes (`src/scenes/`): every `src/scenes/*.ts` exporting `scene: Scene` registers itself (`index.ts`, `import.meta.glob`, sorted by id; no shared edit to add one). `types.ts` holds the contract in its top comment: the host compiles `PRELUDE` (uniforms `res aspect drift time wind ptr ptrVel ptrAmt dark floorY ceilY night sun moon top mid hor glow lit shade`, plus `noise`/`fbm`/`lin`/`gradeLight`/`gradeDark`/`finish`) + the scene's `fragment` lazily, one program per scene in one context; a scene keeps light Y ≥ `floorY` and dark Y ≤ `ceilY` (hue-preserving), ends with `finish()` (1/255 dither), moves with `drift`/`wind`, answers the pointer only where it shows, stays within ~1.3× the sky's cost, and gives a `fallback` gradient. Eight scenes: `sky` (described above), `alpine` (ranges and pines, rose and peach at dawn and dusk), `aurora` (polar night: curtains over snowy ranges, stars; in light, tints reaching mid-sky that follow the clock), `dunes` (knife-edged ranges, airborne sand on `drift` at rest, sand streamers and walking ripples on the wind), `lagoon` (underwater: surface band, god rays, a caustic floor, bubbles), `nebula` (gas, dust lanes, a lens at the pointer), `ocean` (swells and a glitter path; a high veil on the wind; rings at the pointer, which thins the veil and reflects when over the sky), `rain` (fogged glass: bokeh gathered in city blocks, sizes varying by depth, sliding drops, and the pointer wipes a clear streak). After the first paint `precompile()` compiles the unused scenes one per idle slot (`requestIdleCallback`, never within 1 s of input or while the pointer stirs; with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` the driver compiles in the background and a later slot collects it, polled every 100 ms), so a first visit draws without a compile stall (`__sky.compiled` lists them). Each thread shows `sceneFor(id)` (FNV-1a + avalanche of the session id mod the count, `scenes/pick.ts`, `tests/scenes.test.mjs`); no thread (new-task page, drafts, shared channels) shows the sky. `renderTranscript` calls `setThread` via `showThreadScene` in `main.ts`; a change crossfades: the old scene's last frame is copied to `.thread-sky-fade` (2D canvas) and faded out over 300 ms with WAAPI (opacity only, `data-fading` while it runs, `__sky.fades` counts them), instant on the first thread, before `data-ready`, under reduced motion, while paused or without WebGL. The layer carries `data-thread-scene`; `data-scene="sky"` on the workspace still means any procedural scene. `?scene=<id>` pins one scene everywhere (preview); `tests/sky.spec.ts` runs its readability checks for every registered scene, `tests/scenes.spec.ts` covers the pick, crossfade, pin and reduced motion.

## Motion

- Desktop panda motion (`src/brand-mark.ts` + `.css`, `src/star-panda.ts` + `.css`) follows the mobile brand and splash sources in `mobile/src/ui/brand/` and `mobile/src/ui/splash/`. The launch intro must leave input usable immediately and remove itself after its handoff; reduced motion keeps a static mark and skips the intro. Decorative panda parts are hidden from assistive technology. Behavior specs: `tests/desktop-pandas.spec.ts`.
- Motion: subtle and never in the way. `MOTION = true` in `src/motion.ts` drives surfaces (dialogs, toast, panes; menus arrive via `popover-motion.ts` and leave at once) with short critically damped springs: a few pixels of travel, ~120–220 ms of visible movement, exits ~120 ms; input is never gated and every caller keeps an instant path (`MOTION = false` makes the app instant again). CSS transitions are limited to color, background, opacity, box-shadow and `scale`/`rotate` in 100–200 ms (`translate` only for switch knobs: a few composited pixels), timed with `--ds-ease-in` (120 ms, hover in) / `--ds-ease-out` (180 ms) and `ease-out` (defined in `styles.css`); never animate layout (width, height, grid, top/left). A theme switch closes the same gate for two frames (`setTheme` in `theme.ts`), so the palette lands at once. Segment thumbs' size FLIP plays only when the selection changed (`data-segment-last`), never on re-entry or resize. Neither empty state (`Choose a project`, `Open a project to get started`) draws until the first `list_projects` has settled (`projectsListed` in `main.ts`). Nothing transitions on first paint: `design-system.css` turns transitions off under `html:not([data-ready])` (and the first-paint keyframes of `.turn-change-tree` and `.history-loading`, which stays hidden until the gate opens), and `palette.ts` sets `data-ready` two frames after its last startup palette write, and the first wallpaper scene (decoded after that) closes the gate again and reopens it two frames later (`holdStartupGate` in `wallpapers.ts`). Switches (`.ds-toggle`, `.settings-switch`, `role=switch` checkboxes) slide only once armed (`data-armed`, set by one delegated pointerdown/keydown listener in `palette.ts`), so a state set during render never animates; `morph()` keeps `data-armed`, and the token saver panel and a bot's settings pane redraw with `morph()` so their switches survive to slide (`resetFields` in `bots.ts` then matches each kept field to its markup). Menus (`showPopover` in `src/popover-motion.ts`) arrive with opacity and `scale` only, and glass that materializes (`materializeIn`/`materializeOut` in motion.ts: toast, agents flyout) moves only opacity and transform; never an animated backdrop-filter. Segment thumbs slide on `--spring-fast`; disclosure chevrons (sidebar sections, folders, Bots, Changes, file tree) turn with the `rotate` property on `--spring-fast`, and their redraws go through `morph()` (which keeps the shared start of a list that grew or shrank at its end) so the chevron survives to turn; a section-fold heading swaps its count and chevron by opacity over `--ds-ease-in`. A steer bar that appears during a run, and the approval bar going from empty to filled in the open thread (`approvalBarShown`, since the composer rebuilds its host; never on a thread switch), fade up with `settleUnfolded`. A `.support-spinner` fades in only after 150 ms (`support-appear`), so short work never flashes one. A pulse is `state-pulse` (`styles.css`, opacity only, 1.6s, infinite, inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; every state pulse uses it, thinking dots, the saver switch's busy knob and the bots skeleton included and `syncPulses()` in motion.ts starts each on the timeline's zero, so all breathe in phase, and the launcher has its own copy, since its window does not load that sheet; its worker and subagent rows run in `--launcher-team`, from `--ds-hue-team`). State colours ease with `transition: color|background-color var(--ds-ease-out)`, so a kept node fades and replaced markup lands at once; no keyframes on arrival. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes CSS transitions and animations (`styles.css`, `overlay.css`) and motion.ts keeps only a short cross-fade for surfaces (dialogs, toast, panes); `enterView` and `settleIn` play nothing, so a send or a thread switch runs no animation. The live tool trace (`src/trace-motion.ts`, `src/trace-motion.css`) choreographs more: steps blur in, the two newest result cards unfold (older ones fold), the header mirrors the running step with a shimmer, and the turn folds to its summary at the end, with @keyframes and the Web Animations API; the action dock (`src/action-dock.ts`) follows the same rules. Press feedback uses the `scale` property, never `transform`, so a button positioned with `transform` stays under the pointer while pressed. Sidebar task rows are `.task-row` wrappers holding the `.task-item` and its `.task-actions` (the `.task-more` row menu); the selected row has a slim accent bar inside its leading edge (`left: 0`, opacity 180 ms and `scale` 180 ms on `--spring-fast`); row colours ease in over 120 ms and out over 180 ms, each property with its own duration so the press `scale` keeps `--spring-fast-duration`. Shell motion (`src/motion.ts`): a thread switch plays `settleIn()` once, after the real content draws (a remembered page at once, otherwise when the page arrives, never on the loading placeholder): opacity from 0.7 following the spring (~200 ms) with a 4px rise, continuing from what is on screen and at its current speed if one is already running, so rapid switching never dims the transcript again or rises in steps; the GitHub view fades in the same way (opacity only) once `mountGithubHub` has drawn, as do the Settings content once it has drawn and on each section change, the transcript when Settings closes, and the conversation pane on returning to the Conversation view; a sidebar collapse/expand settles `.conversation-pane` from 0.85 (opacity only; the width lands at once; never `.main-pane`, whose opacity would re-composite the header's and dock's backdrop blur). On a first visit (no remembered page) the previous thread's rows stay for at most 150 ms (`holdTranscript` in `main.ts`, `holdDim`/`releaseHold` in motion.ts): `#transcript` turns `inert` and dims to 0.7 (~120 ms, from what is on screen); the page arriving releases the hold into `settleIn` from the live opacity (above 0.9 the hold is cancelled and the page fades from 0.85, so a fast first visit is never a hard cut), and any other exit (error, welcome, shared channel) ends it; after 150 ms the loading line (`.history-loading`) takes over at once (`.after-hold`). The wider roadmap is in `docs/parity-plan.md`.

## Crash recovery

- [docs/crash-recovery.md](crash-recovery.md). `crash_guard::start` (in `run()`'s `.setup`, after the single-instance plugin has let this process go on; in `headless::serve` once the app is built, before its run loop, so setup and the running host are guarded; a `serve` that cannot start calls `notify_exit` and exits 1; a desktop setup that fails after `start` goes through `crash_guard::desktop_startup_failed` (`startup.rs`: "PandaCode could not start: <error>" on stderr, `notify_exit` first, then a blocking rfd error dialog titled "PandaCode could not start" (body: the error as a sentence, then "If this keeps happening, check PandaCode's data folder:" and the folder, or the error alone without one; on Linux, where a nested GTK dialog can deadlock, the body goes to stderr and the first of `zenity`, `kdialog`, `notify-send` in `/usr/bin` or `/bin` (never `PATH`, `wrapper::system_program`) is started detached as a best effort, never waited for, with only "PandaCode could not start." and the error (`startup::note`, no data folder: its arguments show in the process list)), exit 1, never relaunched or reported)) spawns the same binary as `__crash-guard` (dispatched first in `main.rs`) with a stdin pipe (`link.rs`, `spawn.rs`; `writer.rs` never blocks the app). The guard derives its own relaunch target, data dir and wrapper and refuses to run when its arguments disagree (`guard::disagreement`); this is a consistency check against misdirected arguments, not a boundary against a same-user process, which controls the environment those values come from. `start` first removes old reports, both logs, stale temp files and, from both state files, crash times outside `CRASH_WINDOW_MS` and notices older than 90 days or dated more than a day ahead (`prune_old_files`, `state::prune_old`, again about daily while the process runs: from the 15 s check thread, or from `keep_pruning`'s hourly thread when no guard runs) and runs no guard as PID 1 or when systemd runs this `serve` as a service (`systemd.rs`: `INVOCATION_ID` plus either `JOURNAL_STREAM` naming its own stdout/stderr or, only for a `serve` without `--crash-restart`, a parent named `systemd`; `INVOCATION_ID` alone is inherited by anything started under a service; a relaunched `serve` gets neither variable, `relaunch_command` removes `systemd::SERVICE_ENV`); it logs "Crash guard is off: <reason>." for those. `protocol.rs` lines: `exit`, `term N`, `signal N`, `panic …`, `report`, `arm`/`disarm`, `child <pid> <start>`/`unchild <pid>`, `reset` (a dropped line makes the next send, or the 15 s check, resync every agent group, the live setting, the focus state, `interrupted` while it applies, `held` or `shown` (`commands::held_line`) and, once announced, `exit`), `interrupted` (`crash_guard::store_opened` after the database opened, only while a notice is held unshown and it says agents were stopped: the store's `restart_interrupted` count is above zero or the held notice already said so; `reset` never clears it, `shown` does (and `crash_recovery_shown` stops the app resending it, `link::clear_interrupted`), so it belongs only to the notice held now, never a later crash; the guard's next notice gets `agentsInterrupted`), `held <crashed_at> <flags> <report|-> [cause]`/`shown` (the held notice, for the guard to put back if the app dies unshown without `exit`), `focus`/`blur` (`crash_guard::window_focused`, from `on_run_event`'s `WindowEvent::Focused` and `Destroyed`: `focus` while any PandaCode window has focus, `blur` once none has; a macOS bundle relaunch uses `open -n -g`, staying in the background, only when the last line was `blur`; through LaunchServices it gets the login session's environment, while a direct relaunch keeps the guard's). The panic hook writes `panic` and `report` straight to the pipe fd on Unix (`signals::write_raw`), never through the `LINK` lock. The guard also polls the app's pid every 500 ms while the pipe is open (a stray write end inherited before close-on-exec must not stall recovery), and `Process::gone` counts a zombie as gone. Pipe lines are trusted as much as any same-user process (whoever inherited the write end can write); the guard only bounds them: lines over `MAX_LINE` (1024 bytes) are skipped, at most `MAX_CHILDREN` (4096) agent groups are tracked. EOF plus `policy::classify` decides; only a panic or fatal signal within 10 minutes of the death (`FATAL_GRACE_MS`, long enough for a core dump), or a Windows NTSTATUS exception, relaunches, never `exit`/`term` or a silent kill. Loop limit in `policy.rs` (3 crashes in 10 min, or the 2 newest crash records in that window both startup crashes, timed by the first fatal line, within 30 s of launch; only crashes make records, so a run that ended normally between two startup crashes does not break the run). The app respawns a dead guard (checked every 15 s, at most 3 times, retrying a failed spawn; the target cached at first start) and replays its state; a failed first spawn in `start` (`link::connect`, `first_link`) is retried by the same check within the same 3. A stack overflow on a thread without an alternate signal stack (one Rust std did not start, such as WebKit's) writes no `signal N`, so it counts as a kill and is not relaunched. A guard that still sees the app alive after `GONE_TIMEOUT` does nothing. After `exit` the guard leaves at once only on Windows (`guard::LEAVE_AT_EXIT`, so an installer can replace the executable); on Unix it watches until EOF, then stops agent groups still registered and relaunches nothing.
- `guard::conclude` decides from `crash-restart.json` as read when the app died (`state::saved_setting`), and only when that file is missing or unreadable from the live `Watch::armed` (the last `arm`/`disarm`), never from the spawn-time `Config::armed`. `crash_restart_enabled` answers the same way (the file, else the in-memory value), and the Settings switch reads it. It writes a `crash` report only when no panic report exists for this death (`report` line, or, for a death that can be a panic (`policy::may_be_panic`: a `panic` line, SIGABRT or Windows `0xC0000409`), a panic report dated within `PANIC_REPORT_SLACK_MS` before the crash time and not before this run's launch; in both cases only a report whose header carries `Process: <pid>` of the guarded app, `report::written_by`, so a desktop app and a `serve` host never adopt each other's), so one death leaves one report; with no hook report found the guard writes its own, and `hasReport` is whether one was found or written. The crash time (`guard::crash_time`: the first fatal line, kept between launch and now) times the notice, the crash record, the report and `--crash-restart`. `policy::record` keeps only crashes within `CRASH_WINDOW_MS` before the new one. Each mode has its own state file (`crash-guard.json` desktop, `crash-guard-serve.json` `serve`; `state::state_path`), so neither takes the other's notice or counts the other's crashes. The next start of that mode takes the notice out of the file in `crash_guard::start` during setup (`take_notice`, after the single-instance plugin, so a duplicate instance never does): a desktop run set up for a guard holds it in memory (`commands::hold_notice`) and `crash_recovery_take` returns it on every call until the page calls `crash_recovery_shown` (so a page that died before showing it gets it again), one not set up drops it, `serve` prints it. Every load-modify-save of a state file holds an exclusive lock on `<file>.lock` (`files::lock_exclusive`, at most `LOCK_WAIT` 2 s); a start whose clear cannot be saved removes the state file (losing the history), and if that fails too it hands nothing out and keeps the lock for its run (`state::clear_by_removing`, `keep_lock`), so a pausing guard's `replace_pending` fails after `LOCK_WAIT` and it starts nothing, while this process's own later attempts on that file fail at once instead of waiting `LOCK_WAIT`. ## Remote access and headless

- Thread errors in Connect: a dismissal on the host deletes the host's saved row; a viewer's mirrored copy of it stays (the mirror never deletes, and a deleted oldest row would be backfilled anyway). Dismissing on a viewer (`remoteDevice` thread) hides the copy for that app session only, by design: nothing is deleted or journaled, and it shows again after a relaunch. A live error dismissed on a viewer stays hidden when the run's copies arrive (matched by `errorKey` within that run, `runHides`), for that app session too. See Thread errors under Turn card.
- Remote access, PandaCode Connect ([docs/remote-access.md](docs/remote-access.md)), is opt-in: there is no network traffic until GitHub sign-in (device flow). The relay is `relay/`, a Cloudflare Worker with one SQLite Durable Object per account, hibernated WebSockets, and GitHub app-checked sign-in. It forwards only X25519 + XChaCha20-Poly1305 ciphertext. The native side is `src-tauri/src/remote*`: a host answers only devices in its account's presence, and only while "Allow remote control" is on (no per-device pinning; Full access for remote tasks is a separate, default-off host setting). The relay token and device key live in `connect-secrets.json` (0600, `remote/secrets.rs`), never the keychain: macOS re-prompts for keychain items after every unsigned update. `secret_store.rs` shares its file code (`load`/`update`: 0600 temp, fsync, rename, lock file). Requests, responses and events carry `ts`/`nonce` against replay. A task can *run on another computer* as a mirror: an ordinary local session row with `remote_device`/`remote_session`/`remote_synced_id` (schema 26), started by `remote_start_task` (host side `remote/start.rs` finds or clones the GitHub project into `~/PandaCode`). Agent commands for a mirror (`send_message`, `stop_session`, approvals, input, `steer_task`, model/effort) are routed natively by `remote::route`; the queue stays local. Hosts push raw `AgentEvent`s (`agent.events`, one frame per subscriber per 100 ms, text deltas merged, then `run.finished`), which the viewer re-emits as `agent-event` under the local id, and messages are copied with `task.messages` after each run (`remote/mirror.rs`). `Store::workspace_root` refuses a mirror (`This task runs on <name>.`), so git, worktree and file commands never touch the local disk. There is no remote view. Machine orchestration ([docs/machine-orchestration.md](docs/machine-orchestration.md), off by default) lets agents link other computers (`Settings.links`) and launch mirrors as child threads (`machine_threads`) through `pandacode_machine_*` tools in `remote/orchestrate.rs` (composed by `with_machines` beside `with_vm`, never for team tasks; `machine_launch` and `machine_message` ask for approval unless the task runs in Full access, like every PandaCode host tool; the link's access still caps the child thread's mode, and a Read-only link refuses them; `machine_stop` never asks); `mirror::commit` is the one start path for both `remote_start_task` and `machine_launch` and re-checks the link and parent mode at the commit point (`commit_mode`), and settled threads wake their idle parent once with a `MachineThreads` app-origin turn (`launch_app_turn` in `lib.rs`), claimed under the run's admission lock and marked reported in the turn's own transaction (`Store::begin_task_turn_reporting`); due wakes retry at run end, reopen, startup and re-enable (`orchestrate::reconcile`). Every machine tool answers within 45 s (harness tool calls time out at 60 s): link requests live 10 min in `links.rs` apart from the call (an answer nobody waited for wakes the task with a `MachineLink` turn), and `machine_launch` commits the row and spawns `mirror::start` in the same step (`mirror::commit`); a machine thread's `startId` is its own id, so its lost reply follows main's `task.startStatus` path and a start a quit cut off is followed up again after restart (`mirror::load_starts`; `Store::open` keeps such rows instead of turning them back into drafts); a stop while starting ends the thread here at once and sends `task.cancelStart`, and a failed start keeps the thread as `error` (`fail_start`, from `mirror::not_started`). A link counts only while the device hosts or is offline and still has the key it was linked under (`Link::key`); a stored link whose device left the account still serves `read_access`, so `status_of` lists it (`departed`, named from `Link::name`) and Settings offers only Unlink. `machine_launch` takes `machine: "auto"` (`pick_machine`); `catalog::warm` fills linked computers' catalogs on link and presence, off while orchestration is off. A running thread on a host offline past `links::HOST_OFFLINE_AFTER` (5 min) reports `hostOffline` and wakes an idle parent once all its running threads are on such hosts: that turn sets `reported_by` but leaves `reported` 0, so the thread reports normally if it ends later. `claim` reads presence from a snapshot, never the remote lock (it holds the store lock). `remote/loopback.rs` runs the viewer's start path against the host's start logic on two in-memory stores; `remote::bench::live_two_computers` is the documented real run. Wakes are at-least-once: a wake turn that never reached its agent (no assistant or tool message after it) gives its claim back (`orchestrate::restore_wake`, at run end, on a failed prepare and at startup in `recover_wakes`). A parent interrupted by a restart can be woken; one the user stopped cannot, even after a quit right after Stop (`stop_requested`). Machine approvals wait at most `orchestrate::APPROVAL_WAIT` (240 s) on both the MCP host and Codex dynamic tools (`expire_approvals`), and the MCP host ends a call whose client closed the connection, so a late Approve runs nothing. `remote.json` saves take turns under `Remote::saving` and write the current state.
 The UI is `src/remote*.ts`, with mocks in `src/preview-remote.ts`. Builds need `PANDACODE_RELAY_URL` and `PANDACODE_GITHUB_CLIENT_ID`.
- Method registry (`src-tauri/src/remote/methods/`): methods outside `host.rs`'s built-in `Call` enum live one file per feature (`threads`, `composer`, `git`, `github`, `schedules`, `files`, `projects`, `design`, `tools`, `terminal`, `admin`, `team`), each exporting `METHODS: &[Method { name, access, read_only, parse }]` and listed once in `GROUPS` in `methods/mod.rs`. From that declaration alone `host.rs`/`grants.rs` apply the hosting-off refusal, presence and grant checks, the read lane, batching and replay, and `host.hello` (`PROTOCOL` 2) lists the method, its group feature and `limits`. State-changing methods join the ordered lane unless `lane_for` puts them in Start (`github.startTask`, `chat.start`, `team.start`) or Background (slow git, GitHub, checks, recipes, VM, schedule and `btw.ask` calls); a new slow method must be listed there. A method that changes a task's mode enforces Full access itself (`allow_task_mode`). Shell output, bot memory, checks and skill activation are Full only; `slash.list force` and `usage.limits refresh` check Control at run time; `bot.detail` redacts pairings below Full. Full table: [docs/remote-access.md](docs/remote-access.md#registry-methods).
- Headless host ([docs/headless.md](docs/headless.md)): `pandacode serve` (dispatched in `main.rs`, `handle_serve`) runs the same app on an always-on server with no window and no frontend — `setup_common` in `lib.rs` is the desktop startup minus GUI (overlay/tray/appearance/VM watch), `on_run_event` is the shared exit handler (a window close never stops serve; SIGTERM/SIGINT do, with the same 12 s agent deadline), and both build paths share one `app_context()` because a second `generate_context!` re-embeds the Info.plist static. `src-tauri/src/headless.rs` holds the CLI (`--sign-in` device flow on stdout via `remote.rs`'s headless helpers, `--sign-out`, `--full-access`, `--device-name`) and the native twins of the window's real jobs: queue drain (`drain_queue`, mirroring the bot gateway and frontend `dispatchQueue`), pull-request links and limit resumes (`Store::due_limit_resumes`) at run end / on a timer, and a fail-fast for app-action tools. Bots are configured headlessly with `serve --import-bots <file>` (or dumped with `--export-bots`): `headless_bots.rs` deserializes each entry straight into the UI's `BotInput`/`ChannelInput`/`ScheduleInput` and applies through the same constructors (`bots::create/update/set_capabilities`, `channels::add`, `schedule::create`, `guard::add_rule`), matching by name/kind+label/title/pattern so re-applying converges; the wiring then starts added channels live via `channels::start` (channel loops snapshot config at boot, so hand-written rows need a restart).

## Bots and chats

- Bots (docs/bots.md) live in `src-tauri/src/bots*` and `src/bots*.ts`. Each bot owns a workspace project (kept out of the project tree, filter and picker by `userProjects()`); each conversation is an ordinary task with the normal transcript, streaming, queue, composer and model picker, and `run_finished`/`approval_requested` hooks in `lib.rs` route replies and approvals back to channels. Prompts typed in a bot's own conversation go through `bot_message` so the brief, memories and soul apply; the brief folds behind the message's attached context. The Bots sidebar groups Needs you, Pinned, Bots and Hidden; statuses cover approvals/questions, usage limits, work, unread replies and completed runs. Its plus menu offers New bot and New channel. Profile settings (saved as you go) hold identity, an optional soul (personality Markdown, `bots.soul`), harness/model under Brain and permission modes under Rules; a Skills section picks library skills and MCP servers by reference (`bots.skills`/`bots.mcp`, `bot_set_capabilities`), and a project task can start with a bot (`bot_tasks`, `botTaskBrief` in `main.ts`); per-bot external Channels are labeled Connections. Failover can hold a prompt for another model, with decisions above the composer and model-switch transcript markers. Shared bot channels (like `#launch`) have ordered members and a lead; posts route to the lead or up to two @mentioned members with the channel's recent history, replies return as authored rows, assignments and bot messages start queued and turn in progress only when their turn launches (`promote_taken`), and users can stop (including queued assignments), continue or reassign stopped or failed posts; failover keeps a delivery open until the backup run ends (a dismissed hold nothing resumes settles it as failed; Stop drops a held delivery's card), each run end settles the exact request its launch promoted (`shared_channels::Running`; a failed launch puts it back to queued), a bot reset/delete settles its posts as stopped, and member removal or archiving stops pending work. Channel history is paged (`HISTORY_PAGE`). The channel header edits name/members/lead; archived channels are preview-only (no posts or settings until restored). Bot tasks can use `bot_message_bot(to, text)` to message another bot's desktop task; both threads show expandable, status-labeled markers and the reply returns as `Update from <bot>`. Self-messages are rejected and chains stop at three hops. Shared-channel and message tables live in `bots::SCHEMA` and idempotent `bots::upgrade()`; no app storage schema bump. Connections' secrets stay in `secret_store` via `bots/secrets.rs` and never reach the webview; the local web chat server binds to 127.0.0.1 and checks Host.
- Projectless chats ([docs/chats.md](docs/chats.md)): `chat_create` (`src-tauri/src/chats.rs`) makes `~/PandaCode Chats/chat-<id>/`, registers it as a project named Chat and creates the task; a project is a chat by folder location (`isChatProject` in `src/chats.ts`), so `userProjects()` hides it and the sidebar lists its tasks under Chats. After deleting or moving a chat task, `chat_cleanup` forgets the project and removes the folder only if nothing but PandaCode's files remain.

## Voice input

Setup: [voice-input.md](voice-input.md); browser/native verification scope: [voice-input-design.md](voice-input-design.md#verification).

**Modules**
- `voice-capture.ts`: native work and immutable capture transitions, tied to an origin thread (identity: request, capture, thread, generation).
- `voice-dictation.ts`: binds captures to the visible draft, insertion anchors and per-thread send serials.
- `voice-dictation-keys.ts` / `voice-key-actions.ts`: IME-aware key handling. `voice-pill.ts`: card and phase timers; `voice-pill-copy.ts`: copy; `voice-pill-placement.ts`: measured layout.
- `voice-wave-canvas.ts`: canvas size, density, colour and frames; `voice-waveform.ts`: immutable bounded wave state.
- Settings → Voice (own page, `voice` in `settings-sections.ts`): `voice-settings.ts` orchestrates the Input, Transcription and Speech services cards; `voice-mic-settings.ts` (device, mic test, shortcut), `voice-local-settings.ts` (destination, language, model actions; `VoicePreferencesStore` serializes saves), `voice-model-browser.ts`/`voice-model-card.ts` (language filter, sorting, cards); `voice-settings-form.ts` owns markup, validation and hints; `voice-settings-profiles.ts` renders saved profiles.
- `composer-restore.ts`: send-restore for failed sends (puts the rejected draft back before newer text; see Sends).

**Recording lock**
- Starting/recording make the prompt read-only, block selection keys and restore its saved selection on selectionchange, Stop and unlock. Mouse start and waveform clicks focus the prompt without moving that selection.
- Enter stops from the prompt, form, non-interactive card surface or BODY (focus dropped by a closed overlay, none open); Space does the same off the prompt; ⌘⇧D/Ctrl⇧D toggles. Buttons keep Enter/Space.
- Escape cancels startup/recording and discards review only from inside the card; it never discards an error's retained audio (only Discard does) and preserves a transcription in progress.
- Keyboard order: prompt, Stop/Cancel, overflowing Dictation details (tabindex 0 and a labelled region only while overflowing), then Attach; Tab routing is explicit for WebKit, and details handle scroll keys directly.
- Voice guidance is applied after the ordinary composer attributes on every redraw; unlock restores current agent guidance.
- The composer form is focusable (tabindex -1, outline scoped to `#composer[tabindex]`) only while dictation is starting/recording or its card shows.

**Insertion**
- Closing the window while recording: native stops and transcribes the capture, emits a voice-event `transcribing`, then `voice://transcript` `{ captureId, requestId, text }` (a failure is a voice-event `error` with the audio kept for Retry). For its own capture still recording, `voice-capture.ts` treats either event as Stop (`finish()`): the anchor, send holds and review rules apply, and its `voice_stop` joins native's result, so only that reply inserts (never twice); foreign or discarded captures are ignored. Preview: `__voiceSimulateWindowClose({ text?, transcribing? })`.
- Stop saves the caret/selection with the text before and after it. An unchanged draft inserts there. After edits, the place must still be framed on both sides, or the edit must lie wholly before or after it; one-sided context needs at least 8 characters and a unique match. Anything else goes to review.
- Auto-insertion keeps the current typing selection and focus. Explicit Insert at cursor uses the current selection.
- Explicitly clearing a draft (Clear draft, /btw, emptying it by typing) always means review.

**Sends**
- `prepareSubmit` is the recording-lock check: locked sends (including slash instructions and GitHub task starts) leave the draft untouched and show “Stop dictation to send”.
- `beginSubmit` runs at commitment, returns the settlement callback and gives the send a serial. A transcript is held (transcribing) only while a send that began after its anchor is pending, then shows review. Dictation started after a send inserts at its own anchor; one finishing after every send settled inserts at the caret of an empty composer, otherwise review. A committed send clears any send hint.
- Normal, cloud and remote sends commit, then clear their draft synchronously before any await; success never rewrites later typing. Slash-command validation stays before commitment.
- Failure restores the original before newer text with a newline. Rejected drafts for one thread restore in written order and each restores its own copy; only an equal draft typed anew is deduplicated. Committed-draft records are dropped with their thread.
- Empty Enter while transcribing shows a short hint; an empty Send does nothing and keeps the anchor.

**Threads and settings**
- Leaving the origin thread stops recording and transcribes; results stay with the origin. Returning re-attaches: an intact anchor inserts (once the origin's own textarea, by `data-session-id`, is bound and enabled; a redraw first rebinds the outgoing one), otherwise review with the caret restored to the anchor when found. Elsewhere the mic reads “Dictation pending in another thread” and activation hints “Dictation is waiting in “<title>”.” with Go to thread.
- Deleting the origin thread or explicit discard cancels. A failed configuration read never discards; only a successful read that turns voice off or removes the profile cancels startup/recording. Stopped audio and review text always survive settings changes; native work keeps its provider snapshot.
- Retry keeps the endpoint/model/key snapshot; Record again discards it and reads current settings. Failures go through `logVoiceFailure`. A new Start waits for a bounded set of unresolved cancellations.

**Native and privacy**
- Native code owns the microphone, bounded audio, credentials and transport; nothing is requested before an explicit recording, and credentials never reach the webview. `voice_cancel` takes `{requestId?, captureId?}` (at least one; requestId during startup).
- Textarea identity and native undo are preserved. A missing canvas context leaves the read-only prompt visible.

**Local engine** (`src-tauri/src/voice/local/`, IPC contract atop `mod.rs`)
- `transcribe-cpp =0.4.1`, linked statically: Metal on Apple silicon, CPU elsewhere. Release builds never tune ggml to the runner's CPU, via `TRANSCRIBE_CMAKE_ARGS` in `.github/workflows/release.yml` (mirrored in `ci.yml`: the Linux job, and the `rust-platforms` clippy rows for macOS arm64, macOS x64 (Intel), Windows x64, Windows arm64 and Linux arm64, which copy their release rows so platform breaks surface before a release): x86_64 stays on SSE4.2/AVX/F16C (no AVX2+, as with ort), Linux arm64 on plain ARMv8-A (`GGML_NATIVE=OFF`, `GGML_CPU_ARM_ARCH=armv8-a`), and macOS arm64 is pinned portable by the crate itself. Exception: Windows arm64 builds with `GGML_NATIVE=ON`, because ggml requires it with MSVC on ARM and clang-cl can't be selected (cmake-rs already passes `-Thost=x64`, and CMake accepts only one `-T`). Its CPU floor is therefore the `windows-11-arm` runner's (Azure Cobalt 100: ARMv9 with dotprod and i8mm), except that ggml's MSVC probe also tests SVE and SME, which that row pins off with `-DGGML_MACHINE_SUPPORTS_sve=OFF -DGGML_MACHINE_SUPPORTS_sme=OFF` (the cached `check_arm_feature` results in `ggml-cpu/CMakeLists.txt`; same in the `ci.yml` row) because Snapdragon X has no SVE and `platform.rs` doesn't check it. So `platform.rs` checks both with `IsProcessorFeaturePresent` (dot product, and i8mm as feature id 66, `PF_ARM_V82_I8MM_INSTRUCTIONS_AVAILABLE` from Windows SDK 26100) and reports on-device models unsupported (`platformSupported: false`) when either is missing, e.g. on Snapdragon 8cx Gen 1–3. Windows versions too old to know id 66 answer false, which also blocks local models (speech services still work).
- Runs only in a killable child: the app binary with the hidden `__speech-worker` subcommand (`main.rs` dispatches before Tauri). The app never loads a model; a hang or crash kills only the child, and the next request respawns it. A GPU load failure retries on CPU (`TRANSCRIBE_BACKENDS=cpu`).
- Wire (`worker.rs`): child writes the `PCSPEECH` hello, then frames `u32 LE len | u8 kind | JSON header | '\n' | f32 LE PCM`, max 64 MiB. Requests 1 load, 2 transcribe (+16 kHz mono PCM), 3 reset; replies 0x81 ok, 0x82 text, 0x83 err. Child stdout is reserved for frames.
- Timeouts: load 120 s + 30 s per GB of model file (a GPU-path load that fails, crashes or times out gets one CPU-only retry); transcribe (`worker.rs` `transcribe_timeout`) 15 s + audio duration × the model's pace: 3× its benchmark (`secondsPer30s / 30`), at least 2× the audio; 10× the audio when unbenchmarked; 4× that after a CPU fallback. Cancel or timeout kills the child.
- `audio.rs`: chunking for models without long-form (`maxAudioMs/2`, else 40 s), recursive halving on too-long/truncated/repeating output (floor 0.25 s), Silero VAD drops silence (any VAD error → raw audio).
- Idle unload: `unloadAfter` (default `min_15`, `immediately` unloads after each run), checked every 30 s on a `speech-idle` thread. No UI for it yet.
- Model test caps at 30 s, mic test at 10 s; both refuse while a dictation session is active. While the Settings mic test records, it shows a live input level from the `voice://mic-test-level` event (payload `{ level }`). A Stop or Cancel that arrives while Start still waits for permission or the device cancels that Start (it rejects with the cancelled error and closes the device; Stop resolves `{ cancelled: true }`). A model-id cancel also cancels the VAD download that follows the model.
- Closing the window while a Settings mic or model test runs: native stops it and emits `voice://test-ended` `{ kind: 'mic' | 'model', reason: 'closed' }` (`VoiceTestEnded`); that card goes idle without an error and the mic test stops listening for levels. Preview: `__voiceSimulateTestEnded(kind)`.
- `test_recordings.rs`: the Settings test recordings: the microphone test (`voice_mic_test_*`, `voice_mic_devices`) and the model test (`voice_local_test_model`). A dictation refuses to start while either test records ("Finish the microphone test first." / "Finish the model test first.").
- Quit vs hide: when closing the window only hides it, a recording dictation is stopped and transcribed (above); when closing quits the app (Linux, or no menu-bar item) or the app quits, the dictation is cancelled and nothing is uploaded.
- `src-tauri/src/voice/session.rs` holds the dictation session bookkeeping (phases, reserve, claim, cancel); `src-tauri/src/voice/closing.rs` holds what closing the window does and how a later Stop joins a close-started transcription (a Stop on a Failed session re-sends the retained audio).
- The `--echo` protocol worker (`worker::Echo`) exists only in test and debug builds.

**Catalog** (`catalog.json`, vendored; `catalog.rs` drops invalid entries)
- Generated from `@handy-computer/models@0.2.3` (MIT; ported from T3 Code's generator, see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`) by `node scripts/speech-catalog.mjs --source <unpacked package dir> [--check] [--verbose]`; `--check` fails on drift. Tests: `tests/speech-catalog.test.mjs`.
- Kept: ASR role, family in transcribe.cpp 0.4.1, `minEngine` ≤ 0.4.1, root `.gguf`, valid repo/40-hex revision/SHA-256/size. Dropped for licence reasons: non-commercial (`nc`) licences, Moonshine Community-licensed models and anything `REVIEWED_LICENSES` excludes (MedASR: Health AI Developer Foundations terms). Default `parakeet-unified-en-0.6b` must exist and be recommended.
- Licences: every entry carries `license` and `licenseUrl` (the card shows the licence linked to it). SPDX ids map to canonical URLs via `SPDX_LICENSE_URLS`; source licence `other` needs an entry in the reviewed map `REVIEWED_LICENSES` (precise name + URL, or `exclude` + reason), else generation fails. Notices for the engine and catalog source: `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.
- Grades (by error %): < 5 A, < 10 B, < 20 C, < 30 D, else F; A–C get `+`/plain/`-` at ceiling/∛2² and ceiling/∛2. `secondsPer30s = 30 / accelerated factor` (`null` when unbenchmarked).
- Bumping the engine version requires reviewing loader support (the generator refuses otherwise).

**Downloads** (`store.rs`)
- Only on `voice_local_download`: `https://huggingface.co/{id}/resolve/{revision}/{filename}` at the pinned revision. Redirects (≤ 5) must stay HTTPS on `huggingface.co`, `*.huggingface.co` or `*.hf.co`.
- One download at a time; 3 attempts; free space checked against remaining bytes + 64 MiB. Partial data in `<file>.part` resumes with `Range`; cancel keeps it, delete removes model, marker and part.
- SHA-256 verified (cancellable) before rename; mismatch deletes the part. Files 0600 under `<app data>/speech/models/`; a `.verified` marker (`sha:size:mtime:inode`) avoids rehashing.
- The Silero VAD (`silero-vad-v6.2`, ~1.2 MB) follows the first model download; its failure is ignored.
- Closing the window: a recording dictation stops and is transcribed, a starting one is cancelled, and one that is transcribing or awaiting Retry carries on; the Settings mic and model test recordings stop; model downloads keep running. Quitting the app cancels a download and keeps the `.part` file for resume.
- Progress event `voice://local-model` `{modelId, state, downloaded, size, error?}`, ≤ 10/s.

**IPC and preferences**
- Commands: `voice_local_catalog`, `voice_local_download`, `voice_local_cancel_download`, `voice_local_delete`, `voice_local_select`, `voice_local_test_model` (`start`/`stop`/`cancel`), `voice_mic_devices`, `voice_mic_test_start`/`_stop`/`_cancel`, `voice_preferences`, `voice_update_preferences` (`{ patch }`, merged under the config lock; the UI's save path). - `voice_start` without `profileId` routes by the saved destination; the route (model path, language, VAD) is fixed at Start and reused by Stop/Retry.

**Surface**

- Motion (`ds/motion.ts`, pure numbers in `motion-math.ts`): critically damped springs (`springs.press/snappy/smooth`, damping ratio 1), `springs.momentum` (0.8) only after a gesture with momentum, a few points of travel, exits about 120 ms (`EXIT_MS`), first-mount stagger capped at 8 rows (`useStagger`). Reduce Motion: springs jump, slides become cross-fades (`useMotion` gives the right props), layout changes are instant, and a real cross-fade stays (`ReduceMotion.Never`). `PressScale` scales from a `Gesture.Manual` that never activates, so it runs on the UI thread and a busy JS thread can't delay it, while the Pressable underneath keeps accessibility, long press and yielding to a scroll; with Reduce Motion a press dims instead. `usePulseStyle` (`ds/pulse.ts`) shares one Reanimated loop among every pulsing dot and skeleton: it runs while a focused screen shows one, stops with the last, opacity only, off with Reduce Motion. Worklet rule: never call a plain JS function inside a worklet callback, it crashes on the UI thread. `ThemeFade` hit this building `Easing` config inside a `withTiming` callback; build such values at module scope, mark helpers `'worklet'` (`viewer-geometry.ts`, `clamp` in `Toggle.tsx`), and hop back with `scheduleOnRN`.
- Inbox lists (`src/ui/InboxCard.tsx`, used by the Inbox and `Sidebar.tsx`): each FlatList item is one slice of a section's card (`SectionHead`, `CardSlice`), so a section is never one giant view. `TravelCell` is the list's `CellRendererComponent`: a cell moves to its new place on a transform spring and fades up if it goes far. Hard rule: rows using `Link.Menu` (`src/ui/InboxRow.tsx`) must not use `itemLayoutAnimation` or `exiting`, which leave rows blank: `itemLayoutAnimation` remounts a cell's subtree and the native view behind the menu drops its child. So a folding section fades its slices first (`useFolds`, `leaving`), then leaves the data and `TravelCell` closes the gap. `(tabs)/computers/index.tsx` may keep `itemLayoutAnimation` only because its cards have no `Link.Menu`.
- Tab roots have no titles (the tab bar names them): Computers and Settings hide the header (`headerShown: false`, content starts under the status bar; `TabEdges` reads `options.headerShown` and `useScrollEdgeFeed` falls back to the top inset), and the Inbox keeps a slim transparent bar with only native items, New task and search. Margins come from ds `geometry` (`screenMargin` 10, `sectionGap` 10) on every screen, sheet, the composer and `usePagePadding`; don't write literal page margins or section gaps.
- Inbox behaviour: search is a magnifying glass in the bar (`headerSearchBarOptions` with `placement: 'integratedButton'`, `allowToolbarIntegration: false`) that becomes the native field on tap; closing it clears the query; a query opens every section; section order Running, Needs you, Recent is a deliberate owner decision, followed by Unsent and Bots, then Snoozed and Settled folded. `?computer=` (from a Computers card) shows one computer through `filterInbox`, with a clearing chip (`ComputerFilter`). Swipe reveals Snooze and Pin, and a full swipe snoozes (`fullSwipeLabel`: reversible only, none on unsent rows). Approve, Deny and Stop live in the long-press context menu (action sheet off iOS and on replacing rows); Approve shows the command first, Deny answers at once. `needsYouCount` drives the Inbox tab badge (`NativeTabs.Trigger.Badge`) and the app icon badge (`useAppBadge` in `src/ui/push.ts`, needs badge permission, cleared on sign-out); `AttentionTracker` in `(tabs)/_layout.tsx` keeps those re-renders out of the tabs.
- Task screen: the header title is two lines (`TaskTitle`: title, then project and status), a custom view, so the Duo's split view keeps the plain title. One `Stack.Toolbar` item, More: Pause/Resume for a bot, Changes, Pull request and Agents when the task has them, Model & permissions, Task info. Stop is only in the composer (`src/ui/Composer.tsx`), with a confirm once the run is over a minute old (`CONFIRM_STOP_MS`). The status pill (`RunStatus`, `Floaters` in `[session].tsx`) floats above the composer with Working/Stalled/Waiting on you and the current activity; only its text ticks. `RunNotice` is the banner saying why a run isn't running (failed, stopped, limited, with Retry where a prompt can resend), solid because the transcript scrolls under it, until the next run. A turn that changed files ends in a row (`changesOf` in `src/store/turns.ts` reads the message's `turnChanges`, attributed changes only; `ChangesRow` in `src/ui/transcript-rows.tsx` opens Changes; older hosts send none). `src/ui/tray-fit.ts` is the pure fit of the composer tray from measured widths: model shrinks, branch truncates then leaves, then effort and mode drop to icons, and Full access always keeps its name. The keyboard is interactive through react-native-keyboard-controller: `KeyboardProvider` in the root layout, `useKeyboardLift` follows it every frame on the UI thread including a finger drag. It is a native dependency, so changing it needs a rebuild, not just Metro. `DiffView.tsx` draws a file's diff as one fixed-height `Card` around an `InnerCard` with virtualized rows, a gutter and sticky hunk headers.
- Other redesign notes: New task keeps its prompt in the draft store (`NEW_TASK_DRAFT`, `src/store/draft.ts`, cleared on a successful start) and has Cancel and Start in the native header, where the keyboard can't reach them. `ThemeFade` (`src/ui/ThemeFade.tsx`, last in the root layout) fades the page tone over the window, swaps the scheme under it and fades away, because a scheme change flips native bars in one frame; `appearance.ts` calls it through `setThemeFader`. Every way out (Sign out, a refused token, removing this device, account deletion, leaving the demo) clears drafts, snoozes, pins and the outbox: `src/connect/sign-out.ts` holds the order, stopping the connection first and erasing the outbox before the token so an interrupted sign-out can't send old messages at the next sign-in (`tests/signout.test.ts`).
