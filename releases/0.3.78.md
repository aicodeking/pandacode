# Visual progress

## New

- **See what your agent's interface looks like as it builds it.** When an agent works on something with a screen (a web page, a desktop or mobile app, a simulator, a VM or a design artboard), it can post an annotated screenshot after each iteration. Before a UI exists it can post an SVG idea sketch instead. Agents are only asked to do this when they can actually capture the app's own window; they skip it for CLIs, libraries, backend and docs work, and never capture your whole screen.
- **A picture-in-picture timeline in every thread.** Snapshots appear at the top-left of the conversation, iteration 1, 2, 3 and on through the thread's life:
  - **Collapsed**, it's a small pill with fanned thumbnails, the latest title and how long ago it was posted; a dot pulses while the agent works.
  - **Expanded**, it's a glass card with the screenshot, numbered marks (boxes, circles, arrows, pins and spotlights) and a legend underneath. Hover a legend row to light its mark.
  - **A filmstrip** of every iteration, with step, play, a before/after slider, annotations on or off, and a full-size view with its own filmstrip.
  - **Drag it** to any corner; your corner and collapsed state are remembered.
- **Soft motion.** The card morphs from the pill, iterations crossfade, and marks draw in. New iterations glide in when you're on the latest one and show a "New" badge when you're looking back. Everything is instant under Reduce Motion.
- **It stays out of the way.** It starts collapsed in narrow windows, softens while you scroll or type, and fades to just its header when it sits over the conversation text, letting clicks through to the transcript.

## Notes

- Snapshots are stored in PandaCode's app data, not in your project, and are deleted with their task. Each task keeps up to 200 snapshots and 256 MB; **Clear history** in the PiP removes them.
- Team workers and cloud-agent tasks don't post snapshots.

## Known limitations

- The before/after slider looks uneven when comparing a portrait screenshot with a landscape one.
- At the latest iteration the filmstrip can show a partly cut-off thumbnail at its left edge.
